# Adverbs

**Adverbs in English**

Adverbs are words that modify verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs. They often (but not always) end in "-ly."

## **Types of Adverbs**

There are three main types of adverbs:

- **Simple adverbs:** These are single words that modify verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs. They often (but not always) end in "-ly."

    - **Examples:** quickly, slowly, carefully, happily, angrily

    - **Example sentence:** She ran *quickly* to catch the bus.

- **Phrases:** These are groups of words that function together as an adverb.

    - **Prepositional phrases:** These begin with a preposition.

        - **Examples:** in the morning, on the table, with joy

        - **Example sentence:** We went for a walk *in the morning*.

    - **Infinitive phrases:** These begin with the infinitive form of a verb ("to" + verb).

        - **Examples:** to pass the exam, to buy groceries, to catch the sunrise

        - **Example sentence:** He studied hard *to pass the exam*.

- **Clauses:** These are groups of words that contain a subject and a verb, and they modify the main clause of a sentence. They are often introduced by subordinating conjunctions.

    - **Examples:** *when the bell rang, after the movie ends, wherever you like, where she had left them, if it rains, unless I get sick, because his car broke down, since you are here, so that she could get good grades, in order to arrive on time, although it was raining, even though he was tired*

## **Examples of Adverbs**

**Manner**

1. He spoke **softly** to avoid waking the baby.

2. She danced **gracefully** across the stage.

3. The car swerved **dangerously** close to the edge.

4. They sang **loudly** at the concert.

5. He walked **slowly** through the park.

**Time**

1. I'll meet you **later** at the cafe.

2. We're going on vacation **next week**.

3. She'll be arriving **soon**.

4. The movie starts **now**.

5. I saw him **yesterday** at the store.

**Place**

1. The keys are **somewhere** in the kitchen.

2. Put the books **here** on the shelf.

3. We're going **outside** to play.

4. They live **nearby** in a small town.

5. The dog is hiding **underneath** the table.

**Degree**

1. I'm **extremely** tired after that long run.

2. The food is **too** spicy for me.

3. He's **quite** tall for his age.

4. She's **rather** good at playing the piano.

5. The weather is **somewhat** chilly today.

**Frequency - See also** [Understanding Adverbs of Frequency](https://www.notion.so/Understanding-Adverbs-of-Frequency-0efc6a0abd3b493083dc0c9e2f80de36?pvs=21)

1. I **always** brush my teeth before going to bed.

2. She **usually** takes the bus to work.

3. We **often** go for walks in the park.

4. They **sometimes** eat out on weekends.

5. He **rarely** watches television.

**Reason**

1. He couldn't come **because** he was sick.

2. She was late **due to** the traffic.

3. They canceled the trip **owing to** the bad weather.

4. We decided to stay home **since** it was raining.

5. He failed the test **because of** his lack of studying.

**Purpose**

1. She went to the library **to** study for her exams.

2. He worked overtime **in order to** save money.

3. They bought a new car **so that** they could travel more.

4. I'm taking this class **to** learn a new skill.

5. We're having a party **to** celebrate her birthday.

**Condition**

1. **If** you finish your homework, you can watch TV.

2. We'll go to the beach **unless** it rains.

3. **In case** of emergency, call 911.

4. **Should** you need any help, just ask.

5. **Provided that** you pass the test, you can move on to the next level.

**Concession**

1. **Although** he was tired, he continued working.

2. **Even though** it was raining, they went for a walk.

3. **Despite** the challenges, they never gave up.

4. **Though** she was scared, she faced her fears.

5. **While** I understand your concerns, I disagree with your conclusion.

## **Additional Notes**

- Adverbs can also be used to modify other adverbs. For example, the adverb "very" can be used to modify the adverb "quickly" to mean "very quickly."

- Adverbs can be used to create emphasis. For example, the adverb "really" can be used to emphasize the adjective "beautiful" to mean "really beautiful."

- Adverbs can be used to create a sense of rhythm and flow in a sentence. For example, the adverb "then" can be used to connect two clauses of a sentence.
