# Dos and Don't when writing an academic text

[Final Formatting Tips for Microsoft Word Working Towards a Final Draft of an Academic Essay](https://youtu.be/sJMvo468E30)[Final Formatting Tips for Microsoft Word Working Towards a Final Draft of an Academic Essay - Notes](../Weblinks/Final%20Formatting%20Tips%20for%20Microsoft%20Word%20Working%20Towards%20a%20Final%20Draft%20of%20an%20Aca%20(2).md)

- 💡 Review this note when completing a first and final drafts.

# What to do

- When listing three or more items, include a [[Serial or Oxford Comma]].

- Remove all extraneous information (comments in Word, outlines, problem statements, etc.) when submitting first and final drafts.

    - Word comments of any kind: Word comments to yourself or comments with feedback

    - Outline

    - Problem statement

    - Any headings that are not the title of the essay and the *References* heading at the end of the essay

    - Highlighted text or text in bold

- Write in the third person (TP).

    - Avoid first and second person.

- A topic sentence should...

    - Begin with the main (i.e., independent) clause. [5 Types of Clauses with Examples in English Grammar - EnglishBix](https://www.englishbix.com/types-of-clauses-in-grammar/)

    - Avoid a linking verb in the main clause

    - Be a complex sentence and not a compound sentence.

    - Should contain phrases and clauses to make it more specific than the key point listed at the end of the thesis statement

    - Contain a noun as the subject of the main clause (avoiding subject pronouns)

    - Be in the active voice

- Stay mainly in the active voice throughout the essay. [Use the active voice – The Writing Center – UW–Madison](https://writing.wisc.edu/handbook/style/ccs_activevoice/)

- Conclude the instruction paragraph and begin the conclusion paragraph with a thesis statement (Refer to the assignment, [Thesis Statement And Skeleton Outline](file:///C:/Program Files/RemNote/resources/app.asar/build/doc/bwvwibKlM46vFs6F1).

- Include at least three peer-reviewed, academic articles that are cited and referenced according to APA, 7th edition. [Citing Articles - APA 7th Edition - Research Guides at University of Alabama](https://guides.lib.ua.edu/APA7/articles)

- For a five paragraph essay, include two level I headings: 1) the title of the essay and 2) a heading for the references called, "References" - without using quotation marks. A level I heading uses title case ([Definition and Examples of Title Case and Headline Style](https://www.thoughtco.com/title-case-capitalization-1692469)) and is centered to the page and in bold.

- Set margins in Microsoft Word at "Normal": one-inch margins.

- Making sure the ruler in Microsoft Word is set to "inches", add a 0.5 inch indentation for each paragraph using the ruler slider bars.

- Include a French indentation for references. Single space within each reference and double space between each reference.

- Double space entire text, making sure to include equal spacing between paragraphs and between headings and paragraphs; that is, avoid any extra spacing between paragraphs and between headings and paragraphs. Refer to the video below, *Final Formatting Tips for Microsoft Word*.

- Review [What should I include in the first draft of my persuasive essay?](file:///C:/Program Files/RemNote/resources/app.asar/build/doc/NaK9qm1k7K5bnx3do)

- Insert a page break just before the References heading.

- Include a specific thesis statement at the end of the introduction paragraph and at the beginning of the conclusion paragraph (See [Thesis Statement And Skeleton Outline](file:///C:/Program Files/RemNote/resources/app.asar/build/doc/bwvwibKlM46vFs6F1)).

# What not to do

- Avoid words like "important", "essential", "good", "better", "best", "beneficial" etc.

- Avoid "very": [45 Ways To Avoid Using The Word 'Very' - Writers Write](https://www.writerswrite.co.za/45-ways-to-avoid-using-the-word-very/)

- Avoid absolutes: "all", "everyone", "never", "always", "no" etc.

- Avoid phrases like, "obviously", "clearly", "without a doubt", etc.

- Avoid phrases that begin with "it": 1) "it is important", "it is necessary", etc. and 2) "it has been found", "it was shown..." etc. - non-referential "it" in the passive voice. [quizlet.com/88962654/chp 7 nonreferential it and there flash cards/](https://quizlet.com/88962654/chp-7-nonreferential-it-and-there-flash-cards/)

- Avoid "there is/are".

- Avoid [informal phrases](https://www.scribbr.com/academic-writing/taboo-words/), "talking about", "as mentioned before", etc.

- Avoid websites as references.

- Except for perhaps the conclusion paragraph, avoid the future tense in the body and introduction of the essay.

- Avoid idiomatic expressions (e.g., [colloquialisms](https://examples.yourdictionary.com/english-colloquialism-examples.html), [phrasal verbs](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/grammar/b1-b2-grammar/phrasal-verbs), [slang](https://www.oxford-royale.com/articles/introduction-english-slang/), etc.)

- Avoid the pronoun "one", subject pronouns in topic sentences, and overall avoid overusing pronouns of any kind.

- Avoid overusing pronouns overall. The problem with overusing pronouns is when their antecedents are unclear (i.e., pronoun reference). [Pronouns and Antecedents - Writing Commons](https://writingcommons.org/article/pronouns-and-antecedents/)

- Topic sentences

    - Avoid linking verbs in the topic sentence - first sentence of each body paragraph. [Linking Verb: Definition and Examples](https://www.grammar-monster.com/glossary/linking_verbs.htm)

    - Avoid subject pronouns in the main clause of each topic sentence.
