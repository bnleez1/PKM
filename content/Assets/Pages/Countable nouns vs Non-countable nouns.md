# Countable nouns vs. Non-countable nouns

## **Countable Nouns**

- **What are they?** Nouns representing individual items or things that can be counted.

- **Quantifiers:** Used with:

    - **Many/Few:** for large/small quantities (e.g., many books, few chairs)

    - **A few/Several:** for a small number (e.g., a few apples, several pens)

    - **Number + Noun:** for specific amounts (e.g., two dogs, five trees)

- **Examples:**

    1. There are **many** students in the classroom.

    2. I have **a few** coins in my pocket.

    3. She owns **several** pairs of shoes.

    4. He bought **three** new shirts.

    5. We saw **few** birds in the park.

## **Non-Countable Nouns**

- **What are they?** Nouns representing substances, concepts, or things not counted individually.

- **Quantifiers:** Used with:

    - **Much/Little:** for large/small amounts (e.g., much water, little time)

    - **A little/A bit of:** for a small amount (e.g., a little milk, a bit of sugar)

    - **Amount words:** to measure (e.g., a cup of coffee, a liter of juice)

- **Examples:**

    1. There isn't **much** space in the car.

    2. I need **a little** help with this project.

    3. She has **a bit of** patience left.

    4. Can you give me **a glass of** water?

    5. We don't have **much** time.

## **Key Differences with Quantifiers**

| **Quantifier**   | **Countable Nouns**           | **Non-Countable Nouns**             |
| :--------------- | :---------------------------- | :---------------------------------- |
| Many/Few         | Many books, few chairs        | Much water, little time             |
| A few/Several    | A few apples, several pens    | A little milk, a bit of sugar       |
| Number + Noun    | Two dogs, five trees          | A cup of coffee, a liter of juice   |
| Some/Any         | Some friends, any questions   | Some sugar, any advice              |
| A lot of/Lots of | A lot of cars, lots of people | A lot of money, lots of information |

**Important Note:** Some nouns can be both countable and non-countable depending on context (e.g., "chicken" as an animal or meat).

## More examples

**Countable Nouns**

1. I saw **many** dogs at the park today.

2. There are **few** people left in the office.

3. Can I have **a few** cookies, please?

4. He bought **ten** new books for his collection.

5. We only have **a couple of** hours before the deadline.

6. There aren't **many** options left.

7. She has **several** friends coming over for dinner.

8. They own **a few** properties in the city.

9. The store had **no** more shirts in my size.

10. There were **dozens of** people waiting in line.

**Non-Countable Nouns**

1. There isn't **much** milk left in the fridge.

2. I need **a little** more time to finish the report.

3. We don't have **much** money left for the trip.

4. Would you like **a bit of** sugar in your tea?

5. She has **a lot of** knowledge about history.

6. There was **too much** noise at the concert.

7. I only have **a little** patience left.

8. Can you give me **a piece of** advice?

9. There isn't **enough** information to make a decision.

10. We need **a lot of** courage to face this challenge.

**Additional Notes:**

- Remember, some nouns can be both countable and non-countable depending on their meaning and context. For example:

    - **Hair:** (Non-countable) She has long hair.

    - **Hair:** (Countable) There are two hairs in my soup.

- Some non-countable nouns can be made countable by using a container or unit of measurement. For example:

    - **Water:** (Non-countable) We need water.

    - **Glasses of water:** (Countable) I drank three glasses of water.

    - **Rain:** (Non-countable) It's raining.

    - **Raindrops:** (Countable) I see raindrops on the window.
