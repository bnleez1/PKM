# Mastering English Particles: From Phrasal Verbs to Negation

In English, **particles** are small words that have grammatical or functional roles, but they do not change in form. They are typically used in conjunction with verbs to add meaning, or they serve as connectors in sentences. Here are the main types of particles used in English:

### 1. **Prepositional Particles**:

These are prepositions used with verbs to create **phrasal verbs**, changing the meaning of the verb. For example:

- **"Give up"** (meaning to quit)

- **"Look after"** (meaning to take care of)

In these cases, "up" and "after" are particles because they change the meaning of the base verbs "give" and "look."

### 2. **Infinitive Particle ("to")**:

The word **"to"** is used before the base form of a verb to indicate the **infinitive** form. It doesn't have meaning on its own but helps express intention or action:

- **"I want to learn."**

- **"She likes to swim."**

In both examples, "to" is a particle that accompanies the verbs "learn" and "swim."

### 3. **Negation Particle ("not")**:

**"Not"** is used to make verbs negative. It negates the verb it accompanies and can appear with auxiliary verbs:

- **"I am not going."**

- **"She does not like pizza."**

Here, "not" serves as a particle that creates a negative form of the sentence.

### 4. **Modal Particles**:

These particles modify the **mood** of a statement, often expressing **emphasis** or **certainty**. Some common modal particles include **"just"**, **"even"**, and **"only"**:

- **"He just left."**

- **"I only have one book."**

These particles emphasize the verb or another part of the sentence without changing its core meaning.

### 5. **Focus Particles**:

These particles help draw attention to specific parts of a sentence, often used for clarification or focus. Common focus particles include **"only"**, **"also"**, and **"too"**:

- **"She only bought one ticket."**

- **"He also came to the party."**

### 6. **Interrogative Particles**:

In some questions, words like **"do"** or **"did"** function as particles, particularly in **yes/no** questions:

- **"Do you like coffee?"**

- **"Did she call you?"**

These particles help form questions without carrying significant meaning by themselves.

### Summary:

- **Prepositional particles**: form phrasal verbs (e.g., "give up").

- **Infinitive particle**: "to" introduces infinitive verbs (e.g., "to learn").

- **Negation particle**: "not" creates negative verbs (e.g., "not going").

- **Modal particles**: add emphasis (e.g., "just left").

- **Focus particles**: highlight certain words (e.g., "only bought").

- **Interrogative particles**: aid in forming questions (e.g., "Do you like...?").
