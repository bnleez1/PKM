# Rethinking Writing Instruction in the Age of AI


[Rethinking Writing Instruction in the Age of AI: A Universal Design for Learning Approach](../Weblinks/Rethinking%20Writing%20Instruction%20in%20the%20Age%20of%20AI%20A%20Universal%20Design%20for%20Learning.md)

**Author:** Randy Laist, Ph.D.

**Publisher:** CAST Professional Publishing (April 2, 2024)

**Length:** 238 pages

When ChatGPT launched in late 2022, the initial reaction across many educational institutions was an immediate pivot toward panic: policing text, implementing AI detectors, and returning to blue-book in-class essays. In *Rethinking Writing Instruction in the Age of AI: A Universal Design for Learning Approach*, Randy Laist offers a refreshing, proactive alternative. Instead of treating artificial intelligence as an existential threat to academic integrity, Laist positions it as a catalyst to deconstruct, humanize, and revitalize how we teach writing.

The core premise of the book is simple yet profound: **writing is far more than an academic currency or a final product—it is a tool for thinking, an avenue for personal expression, and a mechanism for cognitive development.** By shifting our instructional focus from the final essay to the multi-stage human process of composition, educators can design learning spaces where outsourcing thought to an LLM (Large Language Model) loses its appeal.

---

## Moving Beyond the Panic: Two Pedagogical Paths

Laist explicitly draws a line between defensive teaching and a growth-oriented, inclusive framework.

| Dimension           | The Defensive/Panic Response              | The UDL Response (Laist)                        |
| :------------------ | :---------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------- |
| **Primary Goal**    | Preventing academic dishonesty            | Cultivating voice and critical thinking         |
| **Classroom Focus** | The final, gradable text product          | The transparent, multi-stage writing process    |
| **Teacher Role**    | Evaluator and plagiarism monitor          | Facilitator and collaborative design partner    |
| **Student Agency**  | Constrained by templates and surveillance | Expanded through choice and communal expression |

## Theoretical Grounding: Universal Design for Writing

The first half of the book maps the foundational pillars of **Universal Design for Learning (UDL)** directly onto writing instruction. Laist reorganizes the traditional writing curriculum into three brain-network frameworks:

### Engagement (The "Why" of Writing)

Laist argues that the most effective way to make a prompt "AI-proof" is to make it personally and socially meaningful. When students write inside a transparent, supportive peer ecosystem, writing becomes a vehicle for personal agency. The book offers clear strategies to cultivate intrinsic motivation, giving students choice in topics and embedding authentic, real-world rhetorical contexts.

### Representation (The "What" of Writing)

To write well, students must first process and comprehend information. Laist explores how offering diverse entry points—such as audio, visual media, interactive discussions, and multi-text approaches—helps learners build robust conceptual frameworks before they ever sit down to draft.

### Action & Expression (The "How" of Writing)

Instead of forcing all students down a linear, formulaic path (like the rigid five-paragraph essay), a UDL approach accommodates variability. Laist showcases how providing flexible scaffolding, varying options for outlining, and encouraging alternative mediums for early-stage drafting can accommodate diverse cognitive styles and language backgrounds.

## From Theory to Classroom Practice

The second half of the book moves entirely into practical implementation, providing dozens of hands-on, modular writing activities. Rather than treating writing as an isolated, solitary act, Laist structures these activities across six sequential instructional phases:

1. **Building Community:** Activities designed to lower affective filters and establish a transparent learning culture where drafting and vulnerability are normalized.

2. **Inspiration and Brainstorming:** Unpacking creative methods to generate ideas, spark curiosity, and collaborate on shared themes.

3. **Research:** Guiding students to actively wrestle with information, synthesize sources, and use digital tools intentionally.

4. **Outlining:** Mapping ideas visually or textually to build structural clarity before composition.

5. **Drafting:** Focusing on writing as a "laboratory of the mind," prioritizing the messy translation of thought into language over immediate technical perfection.

6. **Revision:** Reframing editing not as a punitive correction phase, but as a collaborative, reflective craft process.

Throughout these sections, Laist demystifies AI, showing how it can be introduced transparently as a supportive brainstorming partner or formative feedback tool rather than a covert shortcut.

## Key Takeaways for Educators

- **Process Over Product:** When instruction focuses heavily on the incremental steps—brainstorming notes, collaborative peer feedback, reflective journaling, and multiple iterative drafts—the process itself becomes the evidence of learning.

- **A Humanistic Core:** AI can generate grammatically pristine text, but it cannot inject memory, authentic perspective, emotional resonance, or human judgment. Laist reminds educators to design tasks that explicitly demand these uniquely human elements.

- **Immediate Classroom Utility:** The inclusion of ready-to-use handouts, activities, and a structured appendix based on the official CAST UDL guidelines makes this a highly practical handbook for professional development, curriculum design, and teacher training.

## Final Verdict

*Rethinking Writing Instruction in the Age of AI* is an essential text for anyone looking to build a vibrant, transparent, and collaborative community of writers. Laist successfully shifts the conversation from systemic anxiety to instructional opportunity, providing an inspiring roadmap for maintaining human-centered pedagogy in a changing technological landscape.
