# Writing mechanics

I think this is an excellent idea pedagogically. Rather than asking students to "write Paragraph 1," each in-class writing session can have a micro-focus. Students write one paragraph while consciously applying only two targeted writing conventions. By the end of Unit IV (13 body paragraphs), they will have intentionally practiced 26 essential academic writing skills, with repeated feedback from you and peers. This fits well with the goals of your Academic Writing syllabus, which emphasizes human-authored drafting, revision, and APA compliance.

Below is a sequence I would recommend.

Paragraph	Focus 1	Focus 2	APA 7 Reference

1	Create a clear topic sentence	Maintain one controlling idea throughout the paragraph	APA Ch. 4 (Organization)
2	Use present tense when discussing literature	Maintain consistent verb tense	APA §4.12–4.13
3	Correct signal phrases ("Smith (2024) argues…")	Integrate quotations grammatically	APA Ch. 8 (Works of Others)
4	Paraphrase instead of patchwriting	Cite every paraphrase correctly	APA Ch. 8
5	Use commas after introductory phrases	Avoid comma splices	APA Ch. 6 (Punctuation)
6	Correct use of semicolons	Use colons only when appropriate	APA Ch. 6
7	Use transitions between sentences	Employ logical connectors (however, furthermore, therefore)	APA Ch. 4
8	Eliminate wordiness	Prefer concise academic language	APA §4.5 (Conciseness)
9	Maintain parallel structure	Use consistent grammatical forms in lists	APA Ch. 4
10	Use pronouns with clear antecedents	Maintain subject–verb agreement	APA Ch. 4
11	Distinguish restrictive vs. nonrestrictive clauses	Correct use of commas with clauses	APA Ch. 6
12	Maintain bias-free language	Use inclusive language	APA Ch. 5
13	Use active voice when appropriate	Use passive voice only when rhetorically justified	APA §4.13

Suggested assessment

For each paragraph, students complete a brief self-check before submitting:

1. □ I intentionally applied Focus Skill #1.

2. □ I intentionally applied Focus Skill #2.

3. □ I highlighted where I applied each skill.

4. □ I reflected (2–3 sentences) on which skill was more difficult.

This transforms the literature review into a deliberate practice sequence rather than simply a large writing assignment.

One refinement I would suggest is slightly changing the order so that the first six paragraphs focus on sentence-level mechanics (grammar, punctuation, APA citation), the next four on paragraph cohesion (transitions, unity, coherence), and the final three on scholarly style (conciseness, bias-free language, voice). This progression better matches how novice academic writers develop competence and aligns well with the process-oriented methodology described in your syllabus.
