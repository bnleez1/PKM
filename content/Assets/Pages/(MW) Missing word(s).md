# (MW) Missing word(s)

Writing in a second language can be challenging, especially when it comes to producing formal texts. One common issue that English language learners (ELLs) face is the omission of necessary words, which can lead to confusion and a lack of clarity. This article will explore some of the most frequent missing word errors in formal writing and provide strategies to avoid them.

### 1. Missing Articles

Articles ("a," "an," and "the") are small but essential words in English. They help to specify and clarify nouns. ELLs often omit articles, leading to sentences that sound incomplete or ambiguous.

**Example of Error:**

- Incorrect: "She is doctor."

- Correct: "She is a doctor."

**Explanation:** In English, singular countable nouns usually require an article. "Doctor" is a singular countable noun, so it needs the article "a."

**Strategy to Avoid:**

- Always check if a singular countable noun needs an article.

- Remember that "a" is used before consonant sounds, "an" before vowel sounds, and "the" for specific references.

### 2. Missing Prepositions

Prepositions are words that show relationships between nouns or pronouns and other words in a sentence. Missing prepositions can make sentences unclear or grammatically incorrect.

**Example of Error:**

- Incorrect: "She is interested learning languages."

- Correct: "She is interested in learning languages."

**Explanation:** The preposition "in" is necessary to complete the phrase "interested in."

**Strategy to Avoid:**

- Learn common verb-preposition combinations (e.g., "interested in," "depend on").

- Review sentences to ensure all necessary prepositions are included.

### 3. Missing Auxiliary Verbs

Auxiliary verbs (helping verbs) such as "is," "are," "was," "were," "has," "have," and "had" are crucial for forming various tenses, questions, and negatives. Omitting these verbs can lead to incomplete or incorrect sentences.

**Example of Error:**

- Incorrect: "She working on the project."

- Correct: "She is working on the project."

**Explanation:** The auxiliary verb "is" is needed to form the present continuous tense.

**Strategy to Avoid:**

- Familiarize yourself with the structure of different tenses.

- Ensure that auxiliary verbs are included when forming continuous and perfect tenses.

### 4. Missing Conjunctions

Conjunctions are words that connect clauses or sentences. Missing conjunctions can result in fragmented or run-on sentences.

**Example of Error:**

- Incorrect: "She wanted to go to the party she had to work."

- Correct: "She wanted to go to the party, but she had to work."

**Explanation:** The conjunction "but" is needed to connect the two contrasting clauses.

**Strategy to Avoid:**

- Use conjunctions to connect ideas logically.

- Review sentences to ensure they are not missing necessary conjunctions.

### 5. Missing Relative Pronouns

Relative pronouns (who, whom, whose, which, that) introduce relative clauses, which provide additional information about a noun. Omitting relative pronouns can make sentences unclear or grammatically incorrect.

**Example of Error:**

- Incorrect: "The book I read was fascinating."

- Correct: "The book that I read was fascinating."

**Explanation:** The relative pronoun "that" introduces the relative clause "that I read."

**Strategy to Avoid:**

- Identify relative clauses and ensure they include the appropriate relative pronoun.

- Practice using relative pronouns in sentences to become more comfortable with their usage.

### 6. Missing Subjects

Every complete sentence in English requires a subject. Omitting the subject can lead to incomplete sentences.

**Example of Error:**

- Incorrect: "Is going to the store."

- Correct: "She is going to the store."

**Explanation:** The subject "She" is necessary to complete the sentence.

**Strategy to Avoid:**

- Ensure every sentence has a clear subject.

- Review sentences to check for missing subjects, especially in longer texts.

### 7. Missing Objects

Objects receive the action of the verb in a sentence. Omitting objects can make sentences unclear or incomplete.

**Example of Error:**

- Incorrect: "She gave to her friend."

- Correct: "She gave a gift to her friend."

**Explanation:** The object "a gift" is needed to complete the meaning of the verb "gave."

**Strategy to Avoid:**

- Identify verbs that require objects and ensure they are included.

- Review sentences to check for missing objects, especially after transitive verbs.

### 8. Missing Infinitives

Infinitives (to + base verb) are often used after certain verbs, adjectives, and nouns. Omitting the infinitive can lead to grammatically incorrect sentences.

**Example of Error:**

- Incorrect: "She decided go to the party."

- Correct: "She decided to go to the party."

**Explanation:** The infinitive "to go" is needed after the verb "decided."

**Strategy to Avoid:**

- Learn which verbs, adjectives, and nouns are followed by infinitives.

- Review sentences to ensure infinitives are included where necessary.

### Conclusion

Missing words can significantly impact the clarity and correctness of formal texts written by English language learners. By being aware of common missing word errors and implementing strategies to avoid them, ELLs can improve their writing skills and produce clearer, more accurate texts. Regular practice, careful proofreading, and a solid understanding of English grammar rules are essential for overcoming these challenges and achieving proficiency in formal writing.
