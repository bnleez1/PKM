# Verb moods

[Verb Mood: Indicative, Imperative, and Subjunctive | Properties of Verbs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dM0ZsC7PQbo)[Verb Mood: Indicative, Imperative, and Subjunctive | Properties of Verbs - Notes](../Weblinks/Verb%20Mood%20Indicative,%20Imperative,%20and%20Subjunctive%20%20Properties%20of%20Verbs.md)

Mood refers to how a verb expresses a speaker or writer's attitude or intention. It's the flavor or tone you give your words to convey different ideas. While there are varying perspectives on the exact number of moods in English, the most common ones are:

1. **Indicative Mood:**

The most frequently used mood, the indicative is used for statements of fact, opinion, or questions.

- **Examples:**

    - The sun **is** shining.

    - I **believe** in education.

    - **Do** you **like** pizza?

    - She **walks** to work every day.

    - We **will** visit the museum tomorrow.

    - The train **has left** the station.

    - They **were** playing in the park.

    - I **think** it's going to rain.

    - The cat **is** sleeping on the couch.

    - The book **was** very interesting.

1. **Imperative Mood:**

Used for commands, requests, or instructions.

- **Examples:**

    - **Close** the door.

    - **Please pass** the salt.

    - **Don't forget** your umbrella.

    - **Be quiet!**

    - **Let's go** to the movies.

    - **Turn** left at the next intersection.

    - **Call** me when you get home.

    - **Don't touch** the hot stove.

    - **Clean** up your room.

    - **Have** a nice day.

1. [[Subjunctive Mood]]:

Expresses wishes, hypothetical situations, suggestions, demands, or conditions that are contrary to fact.

- **Examples:**

    - I wish I **were** taller.

    - If I **were** you, I wouldn't do that.

    - The doctor recommended that she **take** vitamins.

    - It's important that he **study** for the test.

    - I demand that you **leave** immediately.

    - If only I **had** more time.

    - I suggest that we **meet** at the cafe.

    - It's essential that they **be** prepared.

    - It's high time we **left**.

    - Would that it **were** so simple!

**Additional Moods (Sometimes Included):**

1. **Interrogative Mood:** Used for asking questions. (Often considered a variation of the indicative mood.)

- **Examples:**

    - **Is** it raining?

    - **What** is your name?

    - **Did** you finish your homework?

    - **Where** are you going?

1. **Conditional Mood:** Expresses possibility or a hypothetical situation dependent on a certain condition. (Sometimes grouped with the subjunctive mood.)

- **Examples:**

    - If it **rains**, we'll stay home.

    - I **would** go to the party if I had time.

    - **Should** you need assistance, please call.

    - **Could** you please pass the bread?
