# Subjunctive Mood


💡See also [[Verb moods]].

The subjunctive mood in English is used to express wishes, hypothetical situations, demands, suggestions, or conditions contrary to fact. It’s less common in modern English but still important for conveying certain nuances.

### Key Uses of the Subjunctive Mood

1. **Wishes and Desires**:

    - **Example**: “I wish that he were here.”

    - **Explanation**: “Were” is used instead of “was” to express a wish.

2. **Hypothetical Situations**:

    - **Example**: “If I were you, I would take the job.”

    - **Explanation**: “Were” is used to discuss a hypothetical scenario.

3. **Demands and Suggestions**:

    - **Example**: “The teacher insists that he study harder.”

    - **Explanation**: “Study” is used in its base form after “insists that.”

4. **Conditions Contrary to Fact**:

    - **Example**: “If she were to win the lottery, she would travel the world.”

    - **Explanation**: “Were to win” indicates a condition that is not true.

### Examples in Different Contexts

1. **Expressing a Wish**:

    - “I wish it were summer all year round.”

    - “She wishes that her friend were more understanding.”

2. **Hypothetical Situations**:

    - “If he were taller, he could reach the top shelf.”

    - “If they were to move to the city, they would have more opportunities.”

3. **Making Suggestions or Demands**:

    - “The doctor recommends that she take the medication daily.”

    - “It is essential that he be present at the meeting.”

4. **Conditions Contrary to Fact**:

    - “If I were rich, I would buy a mansion.”

    - “If it were not raining, we could go for a walk.”

### Subjunctive in Set Phrases

- **“God bless you.”**

- **“Heaven forbid.”**

- **“Be that as it may.”**

- **“Come what may.”**

### Differences from Indicative Mood

The indicative mood is used for statements of fact and reality, while the subjunctive mood deals with non-real situations. For example:

- **Indicative**: “She is happy.”

- **Subjunctive**: “If she were happy, she would smile more.”

### Summary

The subjunctive mood is a versatile tool in English that helps express wishes, hypothetical scenarios, and conditions contrary to fact. While it may seem complex, understanding its uses and practicing with examples can make it easier to grasp.

## Main ways to set up the subjective mood

In English, certain verbs, expressions, and conjunctions often set up the subjunctive mood. Here are some of the key ones:

### Verbs

These verbs often trigger the subjunctive when followed by “that”:

- **Advise**: “I advise that he be careful.”

- **Ask**: “They ask that she arrive early.”

- **Demand**: “We demand that he leave immediately.”

- **Insist**: “She insists that he go to the doctor.”

- **Recommend**: “The teacher recommends that he study more.”

- **Request**: “They request that she attend the meeting.”

- **Suggest**: “I suggest that he take a break.”

### Expressions

Certain expressions also set up the subjunctive:

- **It is essential that**: “It is essential that she be on time.”

- **It is important that**: “It is important that he finish the project.”

- **It is necessary that**: “It is necessary that they be informed.”

- **It is vital that**: “It is vital that he be present.”

### Conjunctions

These conjunctions often introduce clauses that use the subjunctive:

- **If**: “If I were you, I would apologize.”

- **Though**: “Though he be rich, he is not happy.”

- **Whether**: “Whether it be true or not, we must investigate.”

### Set Phrases

Some fixed phrases inherently use the subjunctive:

- **God bless you.**

- **Heaven forbid.**

- **Be that as it may.**

- **Come what may.**

These words and expressions help create sentences that express wishes, hypothetical situations, demands, suggestions, or conditions contrary to fact.

# Verbs that trigger the subjunctive mood

### **Complete List of Verbs That Trigger the Subjunctive Mood in English**

In English, the **subjunctive mood** is used to express hypothetical situations, demands, suggestions, or necessity. It is most commonly used in **that-clauses** after certain verbs and expressions.

Below is a **comprehensive list** of verbs that typically trigger the **subjunctive mood**, grouped into **categories based on meaning**:

---

### **1. Verbs of Suggestion and Recommendation**

These verbs are often followed by **that + subject + base verb (without "to")**:

- advise

- ask

- demand

- insist

- propose

- recommend

- request

- suggest

- urge

✅ **Examples:**

- The teacher **suggested that** she **study** harder.

- They **recommended that** he **be** more careful.

---

### **2. Verbs of Command and Necessity**

These verbs express **orders, requirements, or necessity**, which also take the subjunctive.

- command

- decree

- dictate

- direct

- enjoin

- instruct

- mandate

- order

- ordain

- prescribe

- require

- stipulate

✅ **Examples:**

- The judge **ordered that** he **pay** the fine immediately.

- The law **requires that** every citizen **be** registered.

---

### **3. Verbs of Expectation and Urging**

These verbs express **expectation or strong persuasion** and take the subjunctive.

- ask

- beg

- beseech

- plead

- pray

- urge

✅ **Examples:**

- She **pleaded that** he **come** home early.

- They **urged that** she **be** on time.

---

### **4. Verbs of Preference**

These verbs express **preferences or hypothetical desires** and can be followed by the subjunctive.

- prefer

- desire

- wish

✅ **Examples:**

- I **prefer that** she **sit** next to me.

- He **wished that** it **were** true.

*(Note: "Were" is used in the* ***past subjunctive*** *for hypothetical situations.)*

---

### **5. Phrasal Expressions That Also Trigger the Subjunctive**

In addition to verbs, several **fixed expressions** also trigger the subjunctive mood:

- It is essential that...

- It is important that...

- It is necessary that...

- It is crucial that...

- It is imperative that...

- It is advisable that...

- It is best that...

- It is urgent that...

✅ **Examples:**

- It is **essential that** she **be** on time.

- It is **imperative that** they **act** now.

---

### **6. Hypothetical and Contrary-to-Fact Verbs (Using "Were")**

While not part of the list above, the **past subjunctive** is used with the verb **"be"** in hypothetical situations, particularly after:

- If

- As if

- As though

- Suppose

- Imagine

✅ **Examples:**

- If she **were** taller, she could reach the top shelf.

- He acts as if he **were** the boss.

*(Note: "Were" is always used instead of "was" in formal hypotheticals.)*

---

### **Summary of Key Structures**

- **Verb + that + subject + base verb** → (*I insist that she go.*)

- **Expression + that + subject + base verb** → (*It is necessary that he be there.*)

- **Hypotheticals use "were"** → (*If I were you, I would study more.*)

By mastering these verbs and structures, English learners can **correctly use the subjunctive mood** in both spoken and written English.
