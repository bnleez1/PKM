# Discourse Analysis: Revising a Conversation While Preserving Its Original Meaning

You are assisting me with a university Discourse Analysis assignment. I will provide a transcript of an authentic conversation between two or more speakers.

Your task is to create a **slightly modified version of my conversation** that gives me opportunities to analyze Grice's four conversational maxims and the distinction between interactional and transactional talk.

## Step 1. Examine my original transcript

Read the original conversation carefully and consider:

- **Quantity:** Whether speakers provide an appropriate amount of information.
    
- **Quality:** Whether speakers contribute information they believe is true and have adequate evidence for.
    
- **Relation:** Whether contributions are relevant to the conversational context.
    
- **Manner:** Whether contributions are sufficiently clear, unambiguous, brief, and orderly.
    
- **Interactional talk:** Communication primarily intended to establish or maintain social relationships.
    
- **Transactional talk:** Communication primarily intended to exchange practical information or accomplish a task.
    
- **Flouting:** A noticeable departure from a maxim that invites the listener to infer an additional meaning.
    
- **Violation:** A covert departure from a maxim intended to mislead the listener.
    

Remember that observing a maxim is different from flouting or violating it. A misunderstanding, indirect answer, or ordinary error does not automatically constitute a flout or violation.

## Step 2. Create a minimally modified conversation

Produce a revised transcript that remains as close to my original conversation as possible.

Follow these requirements:

1. **Preserve the original context.** Keep the same speakers, topic, setting, general purpose, conversational sequence, and natural speaking style.
    
2. **Make the fewest changes necessary.** Retain original utterances whenever possible. Prefer changing a few words or individual replies instead of rewriting complete exchanges. You may adjust an adjacent speaker's response when needed to make the interaction coherent.
    
3. **Make all four maxims identifiable.** The revised conversation must include at least one reasonably clear opportunity to analyze each of the four maxims: Quantity, Quality, Relation, and Manner. A maxim may be observed, flouted, or possibly violated; all four do not need to be broken.
    
4. **Include meaningful variation.** Where natural and possible, include both interactional and transactional talk, at least one recognizable flout, and an example of a possible violation supported by fictional contextual evidence. Avoid introducing dramatic or implausible changes.
    
5. **If my original already provides examples of all four maxims**, do not simply reproduce them. Make small but meaningful changes to create contrasting examples. For instance:
    
    - Change an ordinary observance of a maxim into a recognizable flout.
        
    - Change a flout into an apparent violation, or vice versa, when context supports the distinction.
        
    - Change a mainly transactional exchange into a more interactional exchange, or vice versa.
        
    - Introduce a brief shift between relationship-building and task-oriented talk.
        
    - Change an indirect response into a more explicit one, or the reverse.
        
6. **Maintain realistic conversation.** Preserve natural features such as informal language, contractions, brief replies, repetitions, and conversational markers. Do not make the dialogue sound like a textbook exercise.
    
7. **Make the surrounding turns useful.** Ensure the turns before and after an important reply give me enough evidence to examine its purpose, possible implied meaning, and the listener's uptake.
    
8. **Do not present invented information as authentic evidence.** If a possible violation requires knowledge that the speaker deliberately concealed, provide a short, clearly fictional context note establishing what the speaker knew. Never claim that the actual participants lied or intentionally misled someone.
    
9. **Do not over-edit.** Keep as many original turns unchanged as possible. If there are unavoidable conflicts between including all four maxims and keeping the conversation natural, prioritize a coherent conversation and explain the limitation without revealing the analytical answers.
    

## Step 3. Format your response

Provide the following sections only:

### A. Revised Conversation — Hypothetical Version

Present the entire revised conversation with numbered turns and the original speaker labels.

Use **bold text** only for words or phrases you have changed or added. Do not identify the maxim or conversational function associated with any turn.

### B. Context Notes (only if necessary)

Provide any brief, explicitly fictional background information needed to interpret the modified dialogue. Do not label particular maxims or tell me whether speakers are flouting or violating them.

### C. Revision Summary

State how many turns were changed and how many were retained. Do not explain which discourse-analysis categories were introduced.
