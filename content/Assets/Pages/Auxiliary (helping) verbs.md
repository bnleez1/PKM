# Auxiliary (helping) verbs

[Auxiliary Verbs (Helping Verbs) | Learn English | EasyTeaching](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lDzd7IR1yvw)[Auxiliary Verbs (Helping Verbs) | Learn English | EasyTeaching - Notes](../Weblinks/Auxiliary%20Verbs%20(Helping%20Verbs)%20%20Learn%20English%20%20EasyTeaching.md)

**Helping Verbs (Auxiliary Verbs) in English**

Helping verbs, also known as auxiliary verbs, work together with main verbs to add meaning to a sentence. They can indicate time, possibility, ability, necessity, and more.

**Types of Helping Verbs**

1. **Primary Helping Verbs ("To Be," "To Have," "To Do")**

    - **To Be:** am, is, are, was, were, be, being, been

        - *Example Sentences:*

            1. They **are** playing soccer.

            2. She **was** happy to see me.

            3. I **am** going to the store.

            4. We **were** surprised by the news.

            5. The cake **is** being baked.

    - **To Have:** has, have, had, having

        - *Example Sentences:*

            1. She **has** eaten breakfast.

            2. They **have** finished their work.

            3. I **had** a great time at the party.

            4. We **have** been studying all day.

            5. He **had** finished the book by then.

    - **To Do:** do, does, did

        - *Example Sentences:*

            1. I **do** not like broccoli.

            2. She **does** her homework every night.

            3. **Did** you see the movie?

            4. They **do** not understand the instructions.

            5. **Does** he know the answer?

2. **Modal Helping Verbs -** [[(MO) Modal verbs]]

    - can, could, may, might, must, shall, should, will, would

        - *Example Sentences:*

            1. I **can** speak three languages.

            2. She **could** play the piano when she was younger.

            3. **May** I borrow your pen?

            4. We **might** go to the beach tomorrow.

            5. You **must** follow the rules.

**Key Points:**

- Some helping verbs can function as main verbs in other contexts (e.g., "I have a dog" vs. "I have eaten").

- Modal verbs always appear before the main verb in a sentence.

- Helping verbs are essential for forming complex tenses and expressing various shades of meaning.

# Deep dive

**The Role of Helping Verbs**

Helping verbs are like the supporting actors in a play; they don't take center stage but are crucial to the overall performance. They perform several key functions:

1. **Expressing Tense:** Helping verbs are essential for forming different tenses, indicating when an action takes place.

    - **Past:** He *had* finished his work before I arrived.

    - **Present:** She *is* studying for her exams.

    - **Future:** We *will* travel to Europe next year.

2. **Indicating Aspect:** They also convey the aspect of an action, showing whether it is ongoing, completed, or habitual.

    - **Progressive (ongoing):** The baby *is* sleeping.

    - **Perfect (completed):** They *have* visited many countries.

    - **Perfect Progressive (completed ongoing):** He *had been* working on the project for months.

3. **Forming Questions and Negatives:** Helping verbs play a crucial role in forming questions and negative statements.

    - **Question:** *Do* you like pizza?

    - **Negative:** He *does not* eat meat.

4. **Expressing Mood:** They help to convey the speaker's attitude or certainty about an action.

    - **Possibility:** It *might* rain tomorrow.

    - **Necessity:** You *must* complete the assignment.

    - **Ability:** She *can* sing beautifully.

**Additional Insights:**

- **Contractions:** Helping verbs are often contracted with other words. For example, *"I am"* becomes *"I'm"* and *"He will"* becomes *"He'll."*

- **Subject-Verb Agreement:** When using helping verbs, ensure they agree with the subject of the sentence. For example, *"He is"* is correct, while *"He are"* is incorrect.

- **Modal Verbs:** These are a special category of helping verbs that express various shades of meaning, such as ability, possibility, permission, and obligation. They include words like *"can," "could," "may," "might," "must," "shall," "should," "will,"* and *"would."*

**Illustrative Examples:**

Let's see how helping verbs can transform the meaning of a sentence:

- **She sings.** (Simple present)

- **She is singing.** (Present progressive, emphasizing the ongoing nature of the action)

- **She has sung.** (Present perfect, indicating the action is completed)

- **She can sing.** (Expressing ability)

- **She might sing.** (Expressing possibility)
