# Non-Finite verbs

Non-finite verbs are an essential part of English grammar, and they play a crucial role in sentence construction. Let's dive into what non-finite verbs are, their history, different categories, and some examples to illustrate their use.

### What Are Non-Finite Verbs?

Non-finite verbs are verbs that do not show tense, person, or number. Unlike finite verbs, which are limited by these grammatical categories, non-finite verbs remain unchanged regardless of the subject or the time of action. They cannot stand alone as the main verb in a sentence but are used in conjunction with finite verbs.

### History of Non-Finite Verbs

The concept of non-finite verbs has been a part of English grammar since its early development. The distinction between finite and non-finite verbs can be traced back to Old English, where verb forms were more complex and varied. Over time, as English evolved, the use of non-finite verbs became more standardized, and their functions in sentences became clearer.

### Categories of Non-Finite Verbs

Non-finite verbs can be categorized into three main types: infinitives, gerunds, and participles.

#### 1. Infinitives

Infinitives are the base form of a verb, often preceded by the word "to." They can function as nouns, adjectives, or adverbs in a sentence.

**Examples:**

- To run is my favorite activity. (noun)

- She has a book to read. (adjective)

- He came to help. (adverb)

Infinitives can also appear without "to," known as bare infinitives, especially after modal verbs and certain other verbs.

**Examples:**

- She can swim.

- I heard him sing.

#### 2. Gerunds

Gerunds are verbs that end in "-ing" and function as nouns. They can act as the subject, object, or complement in a sentence.

**Examples:**

- Swimming is fun. (subject)

- I enjoy reading. (object)

- Her favorite hobby is painting. (complement)

#### 3. Participles

Participles are verb forms that function as adjectives. There are two types of participles: present participles and past participles.

**Present Participles:** Present participles end in "-ing" and describe an ongoing action.

**Examples:**

- The running water is cold.

- She saw a flying bird.

**Past Participles:** Past participles usually end in "-ed," "-d," "-t," "-en," or "-n" and describe a completed action.

**Examples:**

- The broken vase was expensive.

- He has eaten dinner.

### Examples of Non-Finite Verbs in Sentences

To better understand how non-finite verbs function, let's look at some examples in sentences:

**Infinitives:**

- To travel around the world is my dream.

- She needs to finish her homework.

- They decided to leave early.

**Gerunds:**

- Jogging every morning keeps me healthy.

- He dislikes waiting for the bus.

- Her passion is dancing.

**Participles:**

- The crying baby needs attention. (present participle)

- The baked cookies smell delicious. (past participle)

- She found her lost keys. (past participle)

### Functions of Non-Finite Verbs

Non-finite verbs serve various functions in sentences, including:

1. **Noun Functions:**

    - **Subject:** Running is good exercise.

    - **Object:** She loves dancing.

2. **Adjective Functions:**

    - **Modifying Nouns:** The singing bird was beautiful.

    - **Describing States:** The tired worker went home.

3. **Adverb Functions:**

    - **Expressing Purpose:** He went to the store to buy milk.

    - **Showing Reason:** She was happy to see her friend.

### Conclusion

Non-finite verbs are versatile and essential components of English grammar. They allow for more complex and varied sentence structures by functioning as nouns, adjectives, and adverbs. Understanding the different categories of non-finite verbs—infinitives, gerunds, and participles—helps in constructing clear and effective sentences. By recognizing and using non-finite verbs correctly, you can enhance your writing and communication skills.
