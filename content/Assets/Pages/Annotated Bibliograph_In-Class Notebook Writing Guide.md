# Annotated Bibliography: Notebook Guide

**[[Academic Writing Unit III]]** | **[[AW Module 07 Reading Sources Critically]]**

> [!important] Goal
> For each source, write a concise annotation (*between 150-200 words and between 7-10 sentences*) that explains **what the study says, how useful it is, and how it connects to your research**.

---
## For Each Source

At the top of your notebook page, write:

- **Source #**
- **Date**
- **Theme**
- **APA reference**

Then work through four steps:

> [!note] 1. Identify — What is this study?
> Briefly explain:
> - What did the researchers study?
> - Who or what did they study?
> - What was the purpose?
>
> **Useful starters:**
> - *This study investigates...*
> - *The researchers examined...*
> - *The purpose of the study was to...*

> [!note] 2. Summarize — What did they find?
> Select only the findings that are **important for your research**.
>
> **Useful starters:**
> - *The study found that...*
> - *The findings suggest that...*
> - *The authors report that...*
>
> Do not summarize the entire article. Focus on what matters for **your research problem** and **research questions**.

> [!note] 3. Evaluate — How useful is this source?
> Identify at least one important **strength, limitation, or reason the source is useful**.
>
> Ask yourself:
> - Is the source relevant to my research?
> - Is the study credible?
> - Is there an important limitation?
> - Is the context similar to or different from mine?
>
> **Useful starters:**
> - *A strength of this study is...*
> - *A limitation is...*
> - *This source is useful because...*

> [!note] 4. Connect — How does it help my research?
> Explain how the source connects to:
> - your research problem and/or research questions;
> - another source you have read; or
> - an emerging theme.
>
> **Useful starters:**
> - *This study is relevant to my research because...*
> - *This finding agrees with...*
> - *This study differs from...*
> - *Together, these studies suggest...*

## After You Write

Check your annotation:

- [ ] I used the **actual article**, not only the abstract.
- [ ] I explained the study clearly.
- [ ] I included only findings relevant to my research.
- [ ] I evaluated the source.
- [ ] I explained how the source helps my research.
- [ ] I wrote the annotation **in my own words**.
- [ ] My APA reference is complete.

## Build Toward Your Literature Review

After each annotation, add a short **Synthesis Note**.

**Theme:** ______________________________

**Connects with source(s):** ______________________________

**Relationship:**

- [ ] **AGREES** — similar finding
- [ ] **EXTENDS** — adds something new
- [ ] **CONTRASTS** — different finding
- [ ] **DIFFERS** — different context, participants, or method
- [ ] **GAP** — something is still missing

**What might these sources allow me to claim?**

____________________________________________________________

> [!question] Keep Asking Yourself
> **What can I say about my research problem when I consider my sources together?**

---

## Your Writing

> [!warning]
> Your final annotations must be **written by you**.

You may use instructor-approved GenAI to help **discover or map possible sources**, but you must verify the sources yourself.

Do not use AI to draft, rewrite, paraphrase, or polish your annotations.

---

## The Big Picture

**Read → Annotate → Connect → Find Themes → Develop Claims → Write the Literature Review**

> [!tip]
> Your goal is not simply to collect **10 separate articles**. By the end of Unit III, you should begin to see how your sources work **together** to explain your research problem.
