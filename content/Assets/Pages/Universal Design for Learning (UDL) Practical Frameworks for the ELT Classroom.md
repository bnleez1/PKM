- What does UDL typically include? - WOB and Back of Handout

    💡 UDL isn’t extra accommodations; it’s design decisions made early.

    1. **Purpose (Why):** In one sentence, what is this section *for* (how it helps students succeed in the course)?

    2. **What to do / What counts:** List the required behaviors or deliverables (be specific and observable).

    3. **Options (Pathways):** Give 2–3 bounded ways students can participate/submit/demonstrate learning (same standard).

    4. **Supports (How to succeed):** Link or name the tools provided (models, checklist, examples, office hours, templates).

    5. **Timeline (When):** State the rhythm and key checkpoints (deadlines, milestones, typical weekly cadence).

    6. **If things go wrong (Recovery plan):** Clear steps for absence/late work/tech issues + how to get back on track.

- What might a UDL-style syllabus look like? - Methodology

    - Methology

        The course methodology is student-centered and focuses on a step-by-step approach that builds foundational skills progressively. It begins with understanding the purpose and structure of different types of essays, such as descriptive, argumentative, or expository. Students are guided through brainstorming and organizing their ideas using graphic organizers and outlines. Instruction emphasizes the importance of crafting clear thesis statements, developing wellstructured paragraphs with thesis statements, topic sentences, and using evidence to support their arguments. Regular practice in drafting, revising, and editing provides the opportunity for continuous improvement. Peer reviews and teacher feedback are incorporated to refine their writing, while lessons on grammar, vocabulary, and transitions help enhance clarity and coherence.

    - Methodology - UDL Adapted

        The course methodology fundamentally shifts from a singular instructional path to a flexible, student-centered framework grounded in UDL. To provide **Multiple Means of… [Which UDL Principle?]**, the course cultivates a low-anxiety, transparent learning community where the "why" of learning is consistently articulated. Instructors will model practice and reflection, essential for future language teachers. To offer **Multiple
        Means of… [Which UDL Principle?]**, foundational skills (such as paragraph structuring and cohesive transitions) are taught using a variety of modalities—textual,
        visual, and auditory. To ensure **Multiple Means of… [Which UDL Principle?]**, the step-by-step approach to drafting is supported by varied cognitive tools. Students may utilize assistive technologies, graphic organizers, and iterative feedback loops (combining text annotations, audio feedback, and peer reviews) to continuously refine their academic writing.

- Intermediate General English Class

    - Scenario A

        - **Scenario A:** Before reading an article about environmental issues, the class completes a "Brainstorming Map" to activate what they already know about the topic and preview key themes.

    - Scenario B

        - **Scenario B:** For the weekly writing assignment, students can choose to write about a movie they recently watched, a personal hobby, or a current news event that matters to them.

    - Scenario C

        - **Scenario C:** For the final speaking project, students are given the option to deliver a live presentation, record a podcast episode, or create a video with a recorded voiceover.

    - Scenario D

        - **Scenario D:** Students use a digital reading platform where they can independently adjust the font size, turn on text-to-speech, or look up intermediate vocabulary words with a click.

    - Scenario E

        - **Scenario E:** During a grammar review activity, students can choose to type their answers on a shared digital board, write them on a mini-whiteboard, or say them aloud to a partner.

    - Scenario F

        - **Scenario F:** Students use a digital project timeline template to break down a 3-week essay assignment into smaller, manageable steps with clear deadlines for each draft.

    - Scenario G

        - **Scenario G:** A vocabulary lesson on English idioms includes the written phrases, a side-by-side breakdown of the literal vs. figurative meanings, and brief audio clips of the idioms used in natural conversation.

    - Scenario H

        - **Scenario H:** Before starting a timed speaking task, students complete a brief 2-minute reflection to choose one specific strategy they will use if they feel stuck or anxious while speaking.

    - Scenario I

        - **Scenario I:** Students work in stable peer-review pairs to evaluate each other’s introductory paragraphs using a structured rubric focused entirely on constructive, growth-oriented feedback.
