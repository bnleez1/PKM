# (WF) - Word form (part of speech) is incorrect

# Shape-Shifting Words: Understanding the "WF" (Word Form) Code

When you get your essay back and see the letters **WF** written over a word, you might feel a mix of relief and confusion. You look at the dictionary and realize: *"But this word means exactly what I wanted to say!"*

You are right. The meaning is correct. **WF** stands for **Word Form** (or incorrect part of speech). This means you chose the right core concept, but the word is wearing the wrong grammatical shape.

Imagine wearing a swimsuit to a formal dinner. The person is exactly the same, but the outfit does not fit the environment. In English, words change their "outfits"—adding or dropping letters at the end—depending on the job they need to do in the sentence.

Here is a breakdown of why Word Form errors happen and how you can dress your vocabulary correctly.

## The 3 Common "Word Form" Traps

When a word is flagged as WF, it usually means it fell into one of these structural traps:

### 1. The Adjective vs. Adverb Mix-Up

Adjectives describe nouns (things), and adverbs describe verbs (actions). A very common error is using an adjective to describe how an action is done.

- **The Error:** "She solved the math problem very *quick*."

- **The Reality:** "Quick" is an adjective. Because it is describing *how* she solved (a verb) the problem, it needs its adverb outfit: *quickly*.

- **The Fix:** If you are describing an action, look for the "-ly" form of the word.

### 2. The Noun vs. Adjective Confusion

Often, learners memorize a vocabulary word as a noun, but then try to use it to describe something else without changing its grammatical form.

- **The Error:** "He is a very *success* businessman."

- **The Reality:** "Success" is a noun. To describe the businessman, you need the adjective form: *successful*.

- **The Fix:** Pay attention to what comes immediately after the word. If a noun comes right after it, you almost certainly need an adjective.

### 3. The Suffix Guessing Game

English has many different suffixes (word endings) to change a word's form, like *-tion*, *-ment*, *-ity*, or *-ness*. Sometimes learners try to logically guess the ending, creating a word that doesn't actually exist.

- **The Error:** "The *dangerosity* of the mountain is high."

- **The Reality:** While "-ity" is a real noun ending, the correct noun form here is simply *danger*.

- **The Fix:** Never guess a word ending. If you are not 100% sure, check the dictionary to see the accepted spelling.

## How to Fix and Prevent "WF" Errors

To stop making Word Form mistakes, you have to stop memorizing single words and start memorizing entire word families.

### 1. Identify the Job

When you see a WF mark, do not immediately look for a synonym. Instead, ask yourself what job the word is doing in that exact spot in the sentence.

- Is it an action? (You need a **Verb**)

- Is it a person, place, thing, or idea? (You need a **Noun**)

- Is it describing a thing? (You need an **Adjective**)

- Is it describing an action? (You need an **Adverb**)

### 2. Chart the Word Family

When you learn a new word, look up its entire family. English is full of patterns. Writing them out in a chart helps you see how the root word changes shape.

| Noun     | Verb     | Adjective | Adverb      |
| :------- | :------- | :-------- | :---------- |
| Creation | Create   | Creative  | Creatively  |
| Beauty   | Beautify | Beautiful | Beautifully |
| Sadness  | (None)   | Sad       | Sadly       |

### 3. Read for Syntax, Not Meaning

If you struggle with word forms, try reading your sentence backwards, or focus only on the words immediately before and after your target word. This breaks your focus on the *meaning* of the sentence and forces your brain to look at the *structure* (syntax).

- If you see "the," "a," or "an" before the word, a noun is coming.

- If you see "is" or "are," an adjective or noun is required.

Getting a "WF" error means your vocabulary is accurate and your ideas are on point. You just need to make sure your words are wearing the right outfits for the occasion.
