# Collocations in English: The art of natural word pairing

[The smart way to improve your English | Learn Collocations](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RnTpYKOLca4)[The smart way to improve your English | Learn Collocations - Notes](../Weblinks/The%20smart%20way%20to%20improve%20your%20English%20%20Learn%20Collocations.md)

Collocations are combinations of two or more words that frequently occur together in English. These pairings sound natural and "right" to native speakers, while other combinations might seem odd or incorrect. Think of them as the "best friends" of the vocabulary world.

**Why Collocations Matter**

- **Natural Flow:** They make your speech and writing sound more fluent and native-like.

- **Clarity & Precision:** They convey your meaning more accurately, as certain word combinations have specific connotations.

- **Vocabulary Expansion:** Learning collocations is an efficient way to boost your vocabulary and express yourself with nuance.

**Categorization of Collocations**

Collocations can be categorized based on the types of words they combine:

1. **Adjective + Noun:**

    - **Examples:**

        - strong coffee (not powerful coffee)

        - heavy rain (not strong rain)

        - deep sleep (not profound sleep)

        - bright idea (not smart idea)

2. **Adverb + Adjective:**

    - **Examples:**

        - deeply moved (not profoundly moved)

        - highly unlikely (not strongly unlikely)

        - perfectly normal (not completely normal)

        - utterly ridiculous (not totally ridiculous)

3. **Noun + Noun:**

    - **Examples:**

        - a surge of anger (not a rush of anger)

        - a glimmer of hope (not a flicker of hope)

        - a stroke of luck (not a piece of luck)

        - a pang of guilt (not a sting of guilt)

4. **Noun + Verb:**

    - **Examples:**

        - lions roar (not lions shout)

        - bombs explode (not bombs detonate)

        - time flies (not time rushes)

        - alarms go off (not alarms ring)

5. **Verb + Noun:**

    - **Examples:**

        - make a mistake (not do a mistake)

        - take a chance (not grab a chance)

        - catch a cold (not get a cold)

        - do homework (not make homework)

6. **Verb + Adverb:**

    - **Examples:**

        - whisper softly (not whisper quietly)

        - wave frantically (not wave feverishly)

        - argue heatedly (not argue hotly)

        - agree wholeheartedly (not agree completely)

7. **Verb + Expression with Preposition:**

    - **Examples:**

        - burst into tears (not blow up in tears)

        - run out of time (not run short of time)

        - come up with an idea (not find an idea)

        - get away with something (not escape with something)

**Learning Collocations**

- **Reading:** Pay attention to word combinations in books, articles, and other texts.

- **Dictionaries:** Use collocation dictionaries (online or print) to find common pairings.

- **Practice:** Use the collocations you learn in your own speech and writing.

- **Context:** Consider the context in which a collocation is used, as meanings can vary.

# More examples

### 1. Adjective + Noun

- **Strong collocations**: Words that are often used together.

    - **Heavy rain**: We had to stay inside because of the heavy rain.

    - **Strong coffee**: I need a cup of strong coffee to wake up.

    - **High expectations**: The teacher has high expectations for her students.

### 2. Noun + Noun

- **Common pairings**: Two nouns that commonly occur together.

    - **Traffic jam**: We were late due to a traffic jam.

    - **Software program**: He installed a new software program on his computer.

    - **Human resources**: She works in the human resources department.

### 3. Verb + Noun

- **Actions with objects**: Verbs that are commonly used with certain nouns.

    - **Make a decision**: It's time to make a decision about the project.

    - **Take a break**: Let's take a break and come back to this later.

    - **Give a presentation**: She will give a presentation at the conference.

### 4. Noun + Verb

- **Subjects and actions**: Nouns that frequently perform certain actions.

    - **The alarm went off**: The alarm went off at 6 AM.

    - **The sun rises**: The sun rises in the east.

    - **A cat purrs**: The cat purrs when it is happy.

### 5. Verb + Preposition

- **Fixed phrases**: Verbs that commonly pair with specific prepositions.

    - **Depend on**: We depend on our suppliers for timely deliveries.

    - **Believe in**: She believes in hard work and determination.

    - **Look after**: He will look after the children while we are away.

### 6. Adjective + Preposition

- **Descriptive phrases**: Adjectives that pair with specific prepositions.

    - **Interested in**: She is interested in learning new languages.

    - **Good at**: He is good at solving puzzles.

    - **Afraid of**: They are afraid of the dark.

### 7. Adverb + Adjective

- **Intensifiers and modifiers**: Adverbs that modify adjectives.

    - **Deeply concerned**: They were deeply concerned about the situation.

    - **Highly unlikely**: It is highly unlikely that it will rain today.

    - **Bitterly disappointed**: She was bitterly disappointed with the results.

### 8. Verb + Adverb

- **Manner of actions**: Verbs that are often paired with specific adverbs.

    - **Whisper softly**: He whispered softly in her ear.

    - **Run quickly**: She ran quickly to catch the bus.

    - **Speak clearly**: Please speak clearly so everyone can hear you.

### Examples in Context

### Adjective + Noun:

- The **tall building** was visible from miles away.

- He had a **bright idea** that could change everything.

- They made a **quick decision** to leave early.

### Noun + Noun:

- The **coffee shop** on the corner is very popular.

- She works for a **fashion magazine**.

- There was a **power failure** during the storm.

### Verb + Noun:

- Please **take a seat** and wait for your name to be called.

- They decided to **set a date** for their wedding.

- We need to **find a solution** to this problem.

### Noun + Verb:

- **The committee meets** every Tuesday.

- **The clock struck** midnight.

- **The dog barked** loudly.

### Verb + Preposition:

- She **apologized for** her mistake.

- He **insisted on** paying for dinner.

- We need to **focus on** our goals.

### Adjective + Preposition:

- He is **capable of** doing the job.

- She is **famous for** her paintings.

- They are **excited about** the trip.

### Adverb + Adjective:

- The **newly elected** president gave a speech.

- It was a **perfectly timed** joke.

- She was **extremely happy** with the gift.

### Verb + Adverb:

- He **laughed loudly** at the joke.

- She **drove carefully** through the snow.

- They **waited patiently** for their turn.

Understanding and using collocations effectively can significantly improve language fluency and make communication more natural and coherent.
