# Introductory Phrases

Introductory phrases are groups of words that come at the beginning of a sentence, providing context or background information. They can set the stage for the main part of the sentence and are often followed by a comma. Let’s explore different types of introductory phrases with examples for simple, compound, complex, and compound-complex sentences.

### 1. Prepositional Phrases

Prepositional phrases begin with a preposition and provide additional information about time, location, or direction.

- **Simple Sentence**:

    - **In the morning**, I like to jog.

- **Compound Sentence**:

    - **In the morning**, I like to jog, and **in the evening**, I prefer to read.

- **Complex Sentence**:

    - **In the morning**, when the air is fresh, I like to jog.

- **Compound-Complex Sentence**:

    - **In the morning**, when the air is fresh, I like to jog, and **in the evening**, I prefer to read.

### 2. Participial Phrases

Participial phrases begin with a present or past participle and describe a noun or pronoun.

- **Simple Sentence**:

    - **Running through the park**, I felt invigorated.

- **Compound Sentence**:

    - **Running through the park**, I felt invigorated, and **seeing the sunrise**, I felt at peace.

- **Complex Sentence**:

    - **Running through the park**, I felt invigorated because the weather was perfect.

- **Compound-Complex Sentence**:

    - **Running through the park**, I felt invigorated because the weather was perfect, and **seeing the sunrise**, I felt at peace.

### 3. Infinitive Phrases

Infinitive phrases begin with “to” followed by a verb and can act as nouns, adjectives, or adverbs.

- **Simple Sentence**:

    - **To improve my health**, I started jogging.

- **Compound Sentence**:

    - **To improve my health**, I started jogging, and **to relax**, I began meditating.

- **Complex Sentence**:

    - **To improve my health**, I started jogging whenever I had free time.

- **Compound-Complex Sentence**:

    - **To improve my health**, I started jogging whenever I had free time, and **to relax**, I began meditating.

### 4. Gerund Phrases

Gerund phrases begin with a gerund (verb ending in -ing) and function as nouns.

- **Simple Sentence**:

    - **Swimming in the ocean** is my favorite activity.

- **Compound Sentence**:

    - **Swimming in the ocean** is my favorite activity, and **hiking in the mountains** is a close second.

- **Complex Sentence**:

    - **Swimming in the ocean** is my favorite activity because it is refreshing.

- **Compound-Complex Sentence**:

    - **Swimming in the ocean** is my favorite activity because it is refreshing, and **hiking in the mountains** is a close second.

### 5. Adverbial Phrases - (i.e., prepositional phrases as adverbial phrases)

Adverbial phrases modify verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs, providing information about how, when, where, or why something happens.

- **Simple Sentence**:

    - **After the meeting**, we went for lunch.

- **Compound Sentence**:

    - **After the meeting**, we went for lunch, and **before the next session**, we took a short walk.

- **Complex Sentence**:

    - **After the meeting**, we went for lunch because we were hungry.

- **Compound-Complex Sentence**:

    - **After the meeting**, we went for lunch because we were hungry, and **before the next session**, we took a short walk.

### 6. Appositive Phrases

Appositive phrases rename or provide additional information about a noun or pronoun.

- **Simple Sentence**:

    - **A talented musician**, John plays several instruments.

- **Compound Sentence**:

    - **A talented musician**, John plays several instruments, and **a skilled artist**, he paints beautifully.

- **Complex Sentence**:

    - **A talented musician**, John plays several instruments, which he learned as a child.

- **Compound-Complex Sentence**:

    - **A talented musician**, John plays several instruments, which he learned as a child, and **a skilled artist**, he paints beautifully.

### 7. Conjunctive Adverbs See also [Sentence connectors by category](https://www.notion.so/Sentence-connectors-by-category-badaf14e4bcb463f89eca4c65cecf111?pvs=21).

Conjunctive adverbs connect two independent clauses and show the relationship between them.

- **Simple Sentence**:

    - **However**, I decided to stay home.

- **Compound Sentence**:

    - **However**, I decided to stay home; **therefore**, I missed the party.

- **Complex Sentence**:

    - **However**, I decided to stay home because I was tired.

- **Compound-Complex Sentence**:

    - **However**, I decided to stay home because I was tired; **therefore**, I missed the party.

These examples illustrate how introductory phrases can be used to add variety and depth to your writing.
