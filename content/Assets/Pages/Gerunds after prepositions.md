# Gerunds after prepositions

### **Understanding the TOEFL Grammar Item: Gerunds After Prepositions**

The given sentence is:

**"They are looking forward to _____ the museum."**

Answer choices:

1. **visit**

2. **visiting**

3. **visits**

4. **be visiting**

To determine the correct answer, we need to analyze the grammatical structure of the phrase **"looking forward to"**, recognize the correct verb form that follows it, and address common misunderstandings.

---

### **1. Identifying the Grammar Rule: Gerunds After Prepositions**

The key phrase in this sentence is **"looking forward to."** This is a common **phrasal expression** in English, meaning **"to be excited about something that will happen in the future."**

The crucial grammar rule here is that **whenever a preposition (such as "to") is followed by a verb, that verb must be in its gerund (-ing) form**.

- **Correct structure:**

    **looking forward to + gerund (verb + -ing)**

    **looking forward to visiting**

Thus, the correct answer is:

✅ **(B) visiting.**

---

### **2. Explanation of Why Other Choices Are Incorrect**

1. **(A) visit** ❌

    - The word **"visit"** is the **base form** (infinitive without "to"), which is incorrect after a preposition like "to."

    - Learners often mistakenly assume "to" here means an infinitive (*to visit*), but **"to" is actually a preposition**, not part of an infinitive phrase.

    - ❌ *They are looking forward to visit the museum.* (incorrect)

    - ✅ *They are looking forward to visiting the museum.* (correct)

2. **(C) visits** ❌

    - **"Visits"** is the **plural noun form** of "visit," but the sentence needs a verb, not a noun.

    - ❌ *They are looking forward to visits the museum.* (incorrect)

    - ✅ *They are looking forward to visiting the museum.* (correct)

3. **(D) be visiting** ❌

    - "Be visiting" is in the **progressive infinitive** form, which does not follow the structure required after "looking forward to."

    - It does not fit the standard pattern of **"preposition + gerund."**

    - ❌ *They are looking forward to be visiting the museum.* (incorrect)

    - ✅ *They are looking forward to visiting the museum.* (correct)

---

### **3. Common Misunderstandings and Errors**

Many English learners struggle with this type of sentence because of confusion regarding **"to"** and its function. Let’s look at some common misunderstandings.

#### **A. Mistaking "to" as Part of an Infinitive**

One of the most frequent mistakes is assuming "to" in **"looking forward to"** functions the same way as in an infinitive (**to + verb**). However, **"to" here is a preposition, not part of an infinitive.**

- ❌ *They are looking forward to visit the museum.* (incorrect)

- ✅ *They are looking forward to visiting the museum.* (correct)

#### **B. Using the Base Form Instead of the Gerund**

Some learners think **"visit"** should follow "to" in its base form. However, because "to" functions as a **preposition**, it **must be followed by a noun or a gerund (verb + -ing).**

#### **C. Confusing "Look Forward To" with Other Expressions**

Another source of confusion is comparing **"looking forward to"** with similar expressions that **do take the infinitive**, such as:

- **"want to"** → I want to visit the museum. (infinitive)

- **"plan to"** → They plan to visit the museum. (infinitive)

Because these structures use **"to + verb"**, learners sometimes mistakenly apply the same rule to **"looking forward to."** However, **"looking forward to" always requires a gerund.**

---

### **4. Summary and Final Takeaway**

- **Rule**: After **"looking forward to"**, always use a **gerund (-ing form)** because "to" is a preposition.

- **Correct Answer**: *visiting* (Answer B).

- **Common Mistakes**:

    1. Using the base verb (**visit**) instead of the gerund (**visiting**).

    2. Misinterpreting **"to"** as part of an infinitive instead of recognizing it as a preposition.

    3. Using an incorrect form, such as a noun (**visits**) or progressive infinitive (**be visiting**).

To master this structure, students should practice similar expressions that also require gerunds after prepositions, such as:

- **be used to + gerund** → I am used to waking up early.

- **be committed to + gerund** → She is committed to improving her English.

By remembering that "looking forward to" always requires a gerund, English learners can avoid this common mistake and improve their TOEFL grammar skills.
