## REGULATION (EU) 2024/1689 OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL [[Date/2024-06-13]]

**laying down harmonised rules on artificial intelligence and amending Regulations (EC) No 300/2008, (EU) No 167/2013, (EU) No 168/2013, (EU) 2018/858, (EU) 2018/1139 and (EU) 2019/2144 and Directives 2014/90/EU, (EU) 2016/797 and (EU) 2020/1828 (Artificial Intelligence Act)**

## Purpose of the EU AI Act

- Establishes a uniform legal framework for developing, marketing, deploying, and using AI systems across the European Union.

- Promotes human-centred and trustworthy AI while supporting innovation, employment, and cross-border trade.

- Prevents Member States from imposing inconsistent national restrictions unless the Regulation permits them.

## Core Values and Protections

- Aligns AI development and use with EU values, fundamental rights, democracy, the rule of law, and environmental protection.

- Seeks a high level of protection for:

    - Health and safety

    - Fundamental rights

    - Individuals and businesses

    - Democratic institutions

    - The environment

## Need for Harmonised Rules

- AI systems can be deployed across many sectors and circulate easily between EU countries.

- Divergent national laws could:

    - Fragment the internal market

    - Reduce legal certainty

    - Restrict innovation and cross-border services

- Uniform obligations are therefore required for AI developers, importers, providers, and users.

## AI’s Potential Benefits

AI may improve:

- Prediction and decision-making

- Operations and resource allocation

- Personalised digital services

- Healthcare, education, agriculture, and food safety

- Transport, logistics, energy, and infrastructure

- Public services, justice, and security

- Environmental monitoring, biodiversity protection, and climate action

## Risks and Possible Harm

- AI may create risks depending on its application, context, and technological maturity.

- Harm may be:

    - Physical

    - Psychological

    - Social

    - Economic

    - Material or non-material

## Human-Centred Approach

- AI should serve people and increase human well-being.

- Public trust requires AI systems and regulations to reflect EU rights and values.

## High-Risk AI Systems

- Common rules are needed for high-risk AI systems.

- These rules should:

    - Protect health, safety, and fundamental rights

    - Be non-discriminatory

    - Comply with international trade obligations

    - Reflect EU digital-rights principles and trustworthy-AI ethics guidance

## Overall Regulatory Goal

- Create a clear and robust EU-wide framework that:

    - Protects public interests and fundamental rights

    - Supports innovative AI solutions

    - Encourages an AI ecosystem consistent with EU values

    - Enables the free movement of AI-based goods and services across the internal market
