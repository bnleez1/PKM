# Who am I?

I'm Benjamin L. Stewart, an EFL teacher educator and researcher based at the [Universidad Autónoma de Aguascalientes](https://en.uaa.mx/portal/). Think of me as a learning weaver. My work involves exploring the intricate threads that connect teaching practice, learning theory, digital networks (like PLNs!), and the human element of education. With a background in curriculum, instruction, and technology (PhD & MA), I'm fascinated by how we can weave these threads together to create stronger, more vibrant learning experiences for everyone involved.

# My Pattern (Philosophy)

The tapestry I hope we can weave together is one of critical thinking, creative problem-solving, and genuine understanding across differences. My educational philosophy centers on guiding learners to become weavers themselves – moving from following patterns (dependency) to creating their own designs (independence), and ultimately, collaborating on beautiful, complex creations together (interdependence). The goal is a community of educators and learners who aren't afraid to experiment with new threads (**Daring**), openly share their patterns and processes (**Sharing**), and value the contributions of every other weaver (**Caring**).

# Assumptions

The assumptions that I have about education certainly don't apply to everyone, but feel that it does apply to enough educators, trainers, and coaches (including myself) that it warrants reflection that underpins my own educational philosophy.

- My natural tendency is to shy away from any formal planning process; yet, I find that using Notion facilitates the process, allowing me to get the most out of how I approach my teaching and learning practice.

- I put more thought into my own teaching and learning practice if I am more transparent and willing to share. I feel it has more purpose.

    - I remind myself to take my teaching practice serious but not personal.

# Bio

Benjamin L. Stewart is a full professor at the [https://www.uaa.mx/portal/](https://www.uaa.mx/portal/), who teaches a variety of subjects in a bachelor’s degree program in English language teaching. He holds a PhD in curriculum and instructional leadership and a master’s degree in education, curriculum, and instruction: technology. His research interests include open and ongoing professional development for teachers through the cultivation of a personal learning network.
