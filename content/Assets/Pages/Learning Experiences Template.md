# Learning Experience Template

## Purpose

Use this template to design a purposeful, student-centered learning experience aligned with course and unit learning outcomes. The design incorporates **Universal Design for Learning (UDL)** and **responsible GenAI use** while maintaining clear alignment among:

**Learning Outcomes → Content → Learning Experience → Evidence of Learning → Assessment**

A learning experience should require students to actively **use knowledge and skills**, make meaningful decisions, produce evidence of learning, receive feedback, and reflect on their learning.

# 7. Universal Design for Learning (UDL)

UDL should **increase access to the learning without changing the essential learning outcome or lowering expectations**.

## Multiple Means of Engagement

Consider how students can engage purposefully with the experience.

Possible supports include:

- meaningful or authentic contexts;

- appropriate student choice;

- flexible grouping;

- collaboration;

- clearly stated goals;

- manageable levels of challenge;

- opportunities for autonomy;

- connections to prior knowledge and experience;

- opportunities for reflection and self-regulation.

### Preserve the Construct

Before providing an alternative, ask:

> **Does this option provide another pathway for demonstrating the same learning, or does it change what I am assessing?**

UDL should remove unnecessary barriers without removing the productive difficulty necessary for learning.

# 8. Responsible GenAI Use

GenAI should have an **explicit pedagogical purpose**. It should support learning without replacing the thinking, decision-making, or performance students are expected to demonstrate.

## AI Use Level

Select the appropriate level:

- [ ] **No GenAI** — Students complete the experience without GenAI.

- [ ] **Optional GenAI Support** — Students may use GenAI for specified supporting purposes.

- [ ] **Structured GenAI Use** — Students use GenAI at designated stages and according to specific instructions.

- [ ] **Critical GenAI Engagement** — Students analyze, evaluate, verify, challenge, or revise AI-generated material.

- [ ] **Required GenAI Collaboration** — Interaction with GenAI is itself part of the intended learning experience.

---

## Permitted Uses

Students may use GenAI to:

- [ ] Brainstorm

- [ ] Generate questions

- [ ] Clarify concepts

- [ ] Receive formative feedback

- [ ] Compare alternatives

- [ ] Practice language

- [ ] Generate an alternative perspective

- [ ] Revise after producing an initial version

- [ ] Critique AI-generated content

- [ ] Other: __________

## Restricted Uses

Students may **not** use GenAI to:

[Identify tasks or cognitive processes that students must perform independently.]

---

## Cognitive Ownership

Identify the thinking that must remain the student's responsibility:

[Interpretation / analysis / evaluation / argumentation / problem solving / decision-making / original drafting / reflection / other]

Ask:

> **If GenAI performs this part of the task for the student, can I still determine what the student understands or can do?**

If the answer is **no**, AI should not perform that part of the learning experience.

---

## Critical AI Engagement

When GenAI is used, students should move beyond simply accepting its output.

Students may be asked to identify:

- What did the AI produce?

- What was useful?

- What was inaccurate, weak, incomplete, or questionable?

- What did I verify?

- What did I accept?

- What did I modify?

- What did I reject?

- Why did I make these decisions?

- What evidence supports my judgment?

- How did AI affect my thinking or final product?

# 12. Instructor Reflection

After implementation, consider:

- What evidence indicates that students achieved the learning outcome?

- Where did students struggle?

- Which UDL supports increased meaningful access?

- Were any supports unnecessary?

- Did students have sufficient productive challenge?

- Did the evidence actually measure the intended learning?

- If GenAI was used, did it support or replace student thinking?

- Did students demonstrate critical judgment about AI-generated content?

- What should be changed before using this learning experience again?

---

# Final Design Check

Before using the learning experience:

- [ ] Learning outcomes are clear and observable.

- [ ] Activities align with the learning outcomes.

- [ ] Students actively use the required course content.

- [ ] The central task requires meaningful thinking or performance.

- [ ] Evidence of learning is clearly identified.

- [ ] Assessment criteria align with the intended learning.

- [ ] Students have opportunities for feedback and improvement.

- [ ] UDL addresses learner variability without lowering expectations.

- [ ] Multiple means of engagement are considered.

- [ ] Multiple means of representation are considered.

- [ ] Multiple means of action and expression are provided when appropriate.

- [ ] Alternative pathways preserve the assessed construct.

- [ ] GenAI use has an explicit learning purpose.

- [ ] AI permissions and restrictions are clear to students.

- [ ] Essential cognitive work remains the student's responsibility.

- [ ] Meaningful GenAI use is transparent and documented.

- [ ] Students critically evaluate AI output rather than automatically accepting it.

- [ ] Reflection provides evidence of learning, decision-making, or metacognition.
