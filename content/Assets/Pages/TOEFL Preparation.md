# TOEFL Preparation

<iframe title="TOEFL Preparation for UAA" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/NAcMOM7L6ao?feature=oembed" height="113" width="200" style="aspect-ratio: 1.76991 / 1; width: 100%; height: 100%;" allowfullscreen="" allow="fullscreen"></iframe>

This site is dedicated to all things related to the Test of English for the Test of English as a Foreign Language (TOEFL). Start early in your preparation and be consistent so you achieve the minimum score required to graduate. The purpose is to provide English language learners (ELLs) and instructors (who are also ELLs) support for preparing for the Test of English as a Foreign Language ([TOEFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_of_English_as_a_Foreign_Language)).  The primary goal is to provide support in listening, grammar, and reading sections of the paper-based TOEFL, but also includes areas of production in the areas of speaking and writing. Also, additional support will be provided for the four skills more relevant to the [TOEFL iBT](https://www.ets.org/toefl/test-takers/ibt.html). 

### Who needs the TOEFL?

The TOEFL is required for English language learners who want to study in the United States. Refer to the table below, depending on the level of study (Lougheed, 1992).

| Admissions policy | Graduate Humanities | Graduate Sciences | Undergraduate | Technical school |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Acceptable | 550-600 | 500-600 | 500-600 | 450-600 |
| Acceptable, with additional language training and reduced course load | 500-550 | 450-500 | 400-500 | 400-450 |
| Further English training required | Below 500 | Below 450 | Below 400 | Below 400 |

# TOEFL Needs Analysis & Support

- For those English language learners, review the results from prior TOEFL attempts to see which area(s) you need the most practice: listening, grammar, and/or reading (sections of the Institutional TOEFL).
- Create an account in Notion and watch, [*How I use Notion! as a student](https://youtu.be/vtyS2Jcv4Dk). I suggest you use your UAA email to gain additional Notion functionality:* https://www.notion.so/help/notion-for-education.
- Needs analysis: Typically, a diagnostics exam (i.e., listening, structure, and reading sections) is applied to help determine which area(s) to focus on first.
- Institutional (paper-based) TOEFL scores-to-percentage correct:
    - Listening
        - 400 =  30%
        - 450 = 44%
        - 500 = 62%
        - 550 = 76%
        - 600 = 88%
    - Structure
        - 400 =  43%
        - 450 = 55%
        - 500 = 68%
        - 550 = 80%
        - 600 = 90%
    - Reading
        - 400 =  37%
        - 450 = 48%
        - 500 = 63%
        - 550 = 75%
        - 600 = 87%

## Grammar

- Brief review of common grammar points included in the TOEFL
    1. **Subject-Verb Agreement**: The committee *decides* on the new policy tomorrow. See [Subject-verb agreement](https://app.notion.com/p/Subject-verb-agreement-d791c878c80240ccab206ba25a259e34?pvs=21).
    2. **Verb Tenses**: By the time she *arrived*, the meeting *had already started*. [**A comprehensive guide to English verb tenses: Mastering the art of time**](https://app.notion.com/p/A-comprehensive-guide-to-English-verb-tenses-Mastering-the-art-of-time-99a55146cda84523b80160c9129f3b6b?pvs=21) 
    3. **Active vs. Passive Voice**: The book *was written* by a famous author. See [**Active vs. passive voice: A Writer's guide to clarity and impact**](https://app.notion.com/p/Active-vs-passive-voice-A-Writer-s-guide-to-clarity-and-impact-07f63ba2b9c44cb291e9c786890b190f?pvs=21).
    4. **Conditional Sentences**: If I *had known* about the traffic, I *would have left* earlier.
    5. **Modals**: You *must* finish your homework before you go out. See [Modal verbs](https://app.notion.com/p/Modal-verbs-cb3c80eb0188418494f2c34580fcdc7a?pvs=21).
    6. **Gerunds and Infinitives**: She enjoys *swimming* in the ocean. [**Gerunds vs. infinitives: Unveiling the subtleties with the Bolinger Principle**](https://app.notion.com/p/Gerunds-vs-infinitives-Unveiling-the-subtleties-with-the-Bolinger-Principle-17402e07ef39418bafc29f6da4d20ac9?pvs=21).
    7. **Articles**: I saw *a* cat sitting on *the* roof. See [Articles](https://app.notion.com/p/Articles-a9a5c5e952db4562b97f94d09bdb7a11?pvs=21).
    8. **Prepositions**: The keys are *on* the table. See [Prepositional phrases](https://app.notion.com/p/Prepositional-phrases-fbe5d29ec5ad4ddc8dc98552215e21c1?pvs=21).
    9. **Comparatives and Superlatives**: This is the *most interesting* book I have ever read. See [**Mastering comparatives and superlatives:  Level up your English adjectives**](https://app.notion.com/p/Mastering-comparatives-and-superlatives-Level-up-your-English-adjectives-1f96dc5ba5d9488893894024941a799c?pvs=21).
    10. **Relative Clauses**: The man *who* called you is my uncle. See [Relative clauses](https://app.notion.com/p/Relative-clauses-a94eff10a4a741d682cedda22d4721f3?pvs=21).
    11. **Parallel Structure**: She likes *reading*, *writing*, and *jogging*. See [Parallelism](https://app.notion.com/p/Parallelism-da0c13282dd54982868269141df4243f?pvs=21).
    12. **Pronouns**: *They* gave *us* their tickets. See [Pronouns](https://app.notion.com/p/Pronouns-cba7fbef1ae4484692f542ed67d42070?pvs=21).
    13. **Adjective Order**: She wore a *beautiful long red* dress. [Order of attribute adjectives & cumulative vs. coordinate adjectives](https://app.notion.com/p/Order-of-attribute-adjectives-cumulative-vs-coordinate-adjectives-13e4b19081c28015a809ef6659f551f2?pvs=21).
    14. **Quantifiers**: There are *many* apples in the basket. See [Quantifiers](https://app.notion.com/p/Quantifiers-8114085065e847b9b1e8ffbff28471fd?pvs=21) and [Determiners](https://app.notion.com/p/Determiners-f1258875bdf34073bdebf7b4acb6b3ba?pvs=21).
    15. **Conjunctions**: I wanted to go for a walk, *but* it started raining. See [Understanding Conjunctions: A Guide for English Language Learners](https://app.notion.com/p/Understanding-Conjunctions-A-Guide-for-English-Language-Learners-b3a725a0dcd74f77a62bd84b2ef5e97a?pvs=21).
    16. **Direct and Indirect Speech**: He said, “I am going to the store.” → He said *that he was going* to the store. See [Understanding Reported Speech: A Guide for English Language Learners](https://app.notion.com/p/Understanding-Reported-Speech-A-Guide-for-English-Language-Learners-415ea68465f4413f93047eed4922ea4c?pvs=21).
    17. **Punctuation**: Let’s eat, *Grandma!* (Correct punctuation can change the meaning.) [Understanding English punctuation](https://app.notion.com/p/Understanding-English-punctuation-4fd01a986c5a47609e31b6d814334af2?pvs=21).
    18. **Sentence Fragments**: *Because I was tired.* (Fragment) → *I went to bed early because I was tired. See [Sentence fragment](https://app.notion.com/p/Sentence-fragment-6a6eac6449464ac9a62ba18cac754fdd?pvs=21).*
    19. **Run-on Sentences**: *She loves to read she has a huge library.* (Run-on) → *She loves to read, and she has a huge library.* See *[Fixing run-on sentences: A comprehensive guide](https://app.notion.com/p/Fixing-run-on-sentences-A-comprehensive-guide-132fcbaa13134be89a43007dee928451?pvs=21).*
    20. **Subject Complements**: The winner *is* she.

## Listening comprehension

- Many of the [Reading comprehension methods, strategies, and teaching tactics](https://app.notion.com/p/Reading-comprehension-methods-strategies-and-teaching-tactics-f0ef9ae005dd4d3ca33fdb44ac47a70f?pvs=21) also relates to listening comprehension.
