# 100 Common Verb + Preposition Collocations

Verb + preposition collocations can be a challenge for English language learners. Distinguishing between phrasal verbs (where the verb + particle create a new, often idiomatic meaning) - see [The world of phrasal verbs: A unique challenge in English](https://app.notion.com/p/The-world-of-phrasal-verbs-A-unique-challenge-in-English-b1d29a62bb804203ba97aa12da71db85?pvs=21) - and verbs that simply require a specific preposition to connect to their object or complement can be tricky.

As an English language teacher trainer yourself, you'll appreciate how crucial these collocations are for fluency. This list focuses on verb + preposition combinations where the verb generally retains its core meaning, and the preposition serves a grammatical function, linking the verb to the rest of the sentence, rather than forming an idiomatic phrasal verb unit.

Here is a list of 100 common verb + preposition collocations (excluding phrasal verbs), alphabetized by verb:

1. **Account for:** (explain, be responsible for) - _You need to account for your expenses._
2. **Accuse (someone) of:** _He was accused of theft._
3. **Adapt to:** _We need to adapt to the new system._
4. **Add to:** _This will add to our problems._
5. **Adjust to:** _She found it hard to adjust to living alone._
6. **Admire (someone) for:** _I admire you for your courage._
7. **Admit to:** _He admitted to making the mistake._
8. **Agree on (a topic/plan):** _We agreed on the date for the meeting._
9. **Agree to (a proposal):** _She agreed to their terms._
10. **Agree with (a person/opinion):** _I agree with you completely._
11. **Aim at/for:** _They are aiming for a high score._
12. **Apologize to (someone) for (something):** _You should apologize to her for being late._
13. **Appeal to:** _The idea appealed to me._
14. **Apply for (a job/position):** _He applied for the manager position._
15. **Approve of:** _My parents don't approve of my decision._
16. **Argue with (someone) about (something):** _Don't argue with me about this._
17. **Arrive at (a specific place, building):** _We arrived at the station just in time._
18. **Arrive in (a city/country):** _They arrived in Paris yesterday._
19. **Ask about:** _She asked about your health._
20. **Ask for:** _He asked for a glass of water._
21. **Attribute to:** _The success was attributed to teamwork._
22. **Base on:** _The film is based on a true story._
23. **Beg for:** _The child begged for another cookie._
24. **Believe in:** _Do you believe in ghosts?_
25. **Belong to:** _This book belongs to Sarah._
26. **Benefit from:** _Students will benefit from the new library._
27. **Blame (someone) for:** _Don't blame me for your mistakes._
28. **Boast about/of:** _He's always boasting about his achievements._
29. **Borrow from:** _Can I borrow a pen from you?_
30. **Care about:** _She really cares about environmental issues._
31. **Care for (look after):** _He cares for his elderly mother._
32. **Choose between/from:** _You have to choose between these two options._
33. **Collaborate with:** _We collaborated with another team on the project._
34. **Collide with:** _The car collided with a tree._
35. **Comment on:** _Would you like to comment on this report?_
36. **Communicate with:** _It's important to communicate with your colleagues._
37. **Compare to/with:** _You can't compare apples to oranges. / Compare this version with the original._
38. **Compete with/against (someone) for (something):** _They are competing with three other companies for the contract._
39. **Complain to (someone) about (something):** _He complained to the manager about the service._
40. **Comply with:** _All businesses must comply with the regulations._
41. **Concentrate on:** _Please concentrate on your work._
42. **Confess to:** _She confessed to the crime._
43. **Confide in:** _You can confide in me._
44. **Confuse (someone/something) with:** _People often confuse me with my sister._
45. **Congratulate (someone) on:** _I congratulated him on his success._
46. **Connect to/with:** _Connect the printer to the computer. / I feel connected with nature here._
47. **Consent to:** _He finally consented to the plan._
48. **Consist of:** _The team consists of five members._
49. **Contribute to:** _Everyone contributed to the fund._
50. **Convert to/into:** _They converted the garage into a study._
51. **Convince (someone) of:** _I convinced him of my innocence._
52. **Cope with:** _She is learning to cope with stress._
53. **Correspond with:** _I've been corresponding with her for years._
54. **Count on (rely on):** _You can always count on me._
55. **Cover with:** _Cover the food with a lid._
56. **Crash into:** _The bike crashed into the wall._
57. **Deal with:** _I have to deal with this problem now._
58. **Decide on:** _Have you decided on a name for the baby?_
59. **Dedicate to:** _She dedicated her life to helping others._
60. **Depend on:** _Our success depends on teamwork._
61. **Devote to:** _He devoted his time to studying._
62. **Differ from:** _My opinion differs from yours._
63. **Disagree with:** _I disagree with that statement._
64. **Discourage (someone) from:** _They discouraged him from leaving._
65. **Discuss (something) with (someone):** _I need to discuss the plan with my boss._ (Note: _discuss about_ is redundant)
66. **Distinguish between/from:** _Can you distinguish between butter and margarine?_
67. **Dream about/of:** _I dreamt about you last night. / He dreams of becoming a pilot._
68. **Escape from:** _The prisoner escaped from jail._
69. **Excel at/in:** _She excels at mathematics._
70. **Excuse (someone) for:** _Please excuse me for being late._
71. **Experiment with/on:** _Scientists are experimenting with new methods._
72. **Explain (something) to (someone):** _Could you explain this rule to me?_
73. **Focus on:** _Let's focus on the main issue._
74. **Forgive (someone) for:** _I forgive you for what you did._
75. **Hear about/of:** _Have you heard about the new policy?_
76. **Hear from:** _I haven't heard from him in weeks._
77. **Hide from:** _The cat hid from the dog._
78. **Hope for:** _We are hoping for good weather._
79. **Insist on:** _She insisted on paying for dinner._
80. **Interact with:** _He interacts well with children._
81. **Interfere with/in:** _Don't interfere with my work. / Please don't interfere in my personal life._
82. **Introduce (someone) to:** _Let me introduce you to my friend._
83. **Invest in:** _They invested heavily in the stock market._
84. **Involve in:** _Don't involve me in your arguments._
85. **Joke about:** _We joked about our old school days._
86. **Know about:** _What do you know about this topic?_
87. **Laugh at/about:** _Don't laugh at him. / We laughed about the funny situation._
88. **Lead to:** _Smoking can lead to serious health problems._
89. **Learn about:** _I want to learn about different cultures._
90. **Listen to:** _Are you listening to me?_
91. **Look at:** _Look at that beautiful sunset!_
92. **Object to:** _Several people objected to the proposal._
93. **Occur to:** _It never occurred to me to ask._
94. **Operate on:** _The surgeon operated on the patient._
95. **Participate in:** _She participated in the competition._
96. **Pay for:** _Who is paying for the meal?_
97. **Prepare for:** _We need to prepare for the exam._
98. **Prevent (someone/something) from:** _The rain prevented us from going out._
99. **Prohibit (someone) from:** _Students are prohibited from using phones in class._
100. **Protect from/against:** _This cream protects your skin from the sun._

This list covers many common combinations learners encounter. Highlighting these patterns explicitly, as you likely do in your training, can significantly help learners move beyond word-by-word translation and grasp natural English sentence structure.
