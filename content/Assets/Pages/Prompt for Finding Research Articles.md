## Prompt for Finding Research Articles

> I am developing a research proposal related to **English language teaching, English language learning, or applied linguistics**. I need help locating **publicly available, peer-reviewed research articles** that relate to my research problem and that may offer **possible solutions, strategies, interventions, or teaching practices** for addressing the problem.
> 
> Here is my current research focus:
> 
> **Researchable problem:**  
> [Paste your researchable problem here.]
> 
> **Problem statement:**  
> [Paste your problem statement here.]
> 
> **Target audience:**  
> [Paste your target audience here.]
> 
> **Possible participants:**  
> [Paste your possible participants here.]
> 
> Search for **3–5 peer-reviewed research articles** that are publicly available in full text. Prioritize research published within approximately the **last 10 years**, although an older study may be included if it is especially important or directly relevant.
> 
> When searching, consider all of the following:
> 
> - the specific **problem or difficulty** described in my researchable problem;
>     
> - the **educational context**, such as elementary school, high school, university, private language school, online learning, etc.;
>     
> - the **English proficiency level or age group**, when relevant;
>     
> - my **possible participants**;
>     
> - my **target audience**, especially the people who could use the findings;
>     
> - possible **solutions, strategies, interventions, materials, classroom practices, or approaches** that have been studied as ways to address the problem.
>     
> 
> Do not limit the search to articles that use exactly the same wording as my problem. Identify related academic terminology and synonyms that researchers may use. For example, a problem described as "students do not want to speak English" might appear in research as _willingness to communicate, L2 anxiety, classroom participation, speaking apprehension,_ or _L1 use in EFL classrooms_.
> 
> For each article, provide:
> 
> 1. **Complete APA 7 reference**
>
> 2. **DOI**, if available
>     
> 3. **Publicly accessible full-text link**
>     
> 4. **Research problem or purpose**
>     
> 5. **Context and participants**
>     
> 6. **Strategy, intervention, or possible solution investigated**
>     
> 7. **Main findings**
>     
> 8. **How the article connects to my researchable problem**
>     
> 9. **How closely the participants match my possible participants**
>     
> 10. **How the findings could be useful to my target audience**
>     
> 
> At the end, rank the articles from **strongest to weakest match** for my proposed research and briefly explain the ranking.
> 
> **Important source requirements:** Only recommend articles that you can verify actually exist. Do not invent titles, authors, journals, DOIs, findings, or URLs. The article must be **peer reviewed** and the full text must be **publicly accessible**. If you cannot verify that an article meets these requirements, do not include it.
> 
> Finally, provide **5–8 search terms or Boolean search strings** that I could use myself in **Google Scholar, ERIC, DOAJ, SciELO, Redalyc, or similar academic databases** to find additional research.
> 
> Do not change my researchable problem or problem statement. Your purpose is to help me **map the existing literature and identify research-based approaches that could potentially address the problem**, not to decide what my study should conclude.

### Suggested follow-up

After getting results, **select the three strongest articles themselves** for the _Article 1, Article 2, and Article 3_ columns in [Academic Writing Matrix.xlsx](https://eduuaa.sharepoint.com/:x:/s/AcademicWriting2026/IQDswUes_hBpSK4zkSAVjdbiAcmC9uuyRwFZrz5vaeFEhns?e=bTi1Cy) rather than simply accepting the AI's first three recommendations. They should open each article and verify the authors, publication, peer-review status, participants, methodology, and findings before adding it to the matrix. This is particularly consistent with the syllabus requirement that students independently fact-check and verify sources discovered with AI.

Focus on articles that investigate **what has been tried to address the problem**, rather than collecting three articles that merely confirm that the problem exists.
