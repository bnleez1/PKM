# New Word

Act as an expert English language teacher trainer and applied linguist. I am building a public-facing course glossary. I need you to generate the rich, block-level content for a specific term, written in an engaging, transparent tone that helps bridge theory and practice for my learners and a wider community of educators.

Term: [Insert Term Here]
Formal Definition: [Insert Basic Definition Here, if you have one]
Subject/Course Context: [Insert Course Name, e.g., Discourse Analysis]

Please generate a comprehensive, structured guide for this term using the following markdown headings. Ensure the content is highly scannable and practical.

1. Expanded Context
Go beyond the basic dictionary definition. Explain the theoretical background, the nuances of the term, and exactly why this concept matters in language education.

2. Common Misconceptions
Identify 1 or 2 frequent misunderstandings that trainees or teachers often have regarding this term. Provide a clear, gentle correction to realign their understanding.

3. Strategies for Analysis
Provide actionable frameworks or questions my trainees can use to identify, analyze, or evaluate this concept when looking at real language data, texts, or classroom discourse.

4. In-Practice Classroom Examples
Give a concrete, realistic scenario of what this concept looks like when actively applied in an English language classroom.

5. Textbook Connection (Writing & Research)
Briefly explore how this concept intersects with academic writing or research methodologies. Provide a drafted thought or connection that I can later expand upon for my upcoming textbook.

6. Media & Representation Ideas
Suggest 1 or 2 specific ideas for a diagram, chart, or brief audio clip I could create to represent this concept visually or auditorily to support diverse learning needs.

### How to use this in your workflow

- **Create a template:** In Capacities, you can create a "Template" for your Concept object type. You can paste the bare headings (Expanded Context, Common Misconceptions, etc.) directly into that template so every new word automatically has the skeleton ready to go.

- **Iterate on the textbook connections:** The textbook section in the prompt is designed to serve as a springboard. When you eventually sit down to draft your chapters on writing and research methodologies, you can filter your Capacities database by that specific heading and immediately pull up dozens of pre-connected ideas.

---

## Rich Content (The Main Body)

The actual "Blocks" body of the Capacities object is where you can bring the term to life. Instead of making these structural properties, reserve the main body space for:

- **In-Practice Examples:** Concrete examples of what the concept looks like in a classroom setting.

- **Audio/Visual Media:** Embedded diagrams, charts, or a quick audio file of you explaining the concept.

- **Textbook Connections:** If the term is a major focus for your upcoming textbook on writing and research methodologies, you can draft those expanded thoughts right in the body of the term.

## Pro-Tip for the Layout

While in the **Object settings**, look for the **Page Layout** option. Set it to **Encyclopedia**. This layout takes all the properties you just created (Course, Spanish Equivalent, Theme) and neatly stacks them in a right-hand sidebar. This keeps the main body of the page clean and visually uncluttered for your trainees, ensuring the focus remains on the core explanation and examples.
