# Reading Strategies for Improving Reading Comprehension

Here’s a list of **reading strategies** you can practice with your English language learners to help them strengthen comprehension skills. I’ve grouped them by **before, during, and after reading** so you can apply them flexibly depending on your lesson goals.

---

## **Before Reading**

1. **Previewing & Predicting** – Skim titles, headings, pictures, and key words; have learners guess what the text might be about.

2. **Activating Prior Knowledge** – Ask learners what they already know about the topic and connect it to their personal experiences.

3. **Setting a Purpose for Reading** – Encourage students to read for a specific purpose (e.g., “find the problem,” “understand the writer’s opinion”).

4. **Pre-teaching Vocabulary** – Introduce a handful of key words in context so learners won’t get stuck on every unknown word.

---

## **During Reading**

1. **Skimming** – Practice reading quickly to get the main idea without focusing on every word.

2. **Scanning** – Train learners to look for specific details like names, numbers, or dates.

3. **Identifying Main Ideas & Supporting Details** – Use guiding questions or graphic organizers to separate big ideas from examples.

4. **Making Predictions** – Pause midway and ask learners to guess what comes next, then check predictions as they continue reading.

5. **Monitoring Comprehension (Self-Questioning)** – Encourage learners to stop and ask themselves: *“Do I understand this? What’s confusing?”*

6. **Context Clues** – Teach strategies for guessing the meaning of unknown words from surrounding words or sentences.

7. **Highlighting/Annotating** – Have learners underline key sentences, circle words, or write short margin notes.

8. **Visualizing** – Guide learners to create mental images of what they read to reinforce memory and comprehension.

---

## **After Reading**

1. **Summarizing** – Learners retell the main points in their own words, orally or in writing.

2. **Retelling or Story Mapping** – Use story maps, timelines, or charts to recall events and sequence.

3. **Question Generation** – Learners create questions about the text for peers to answer (helps them read more critically).

4. **Discussing Author’s Purpose & Tone** – Encourage learners to reflect on why the author wrote the text and what attitude is shown.

5. **Making Inferences** – Practice reading between the lines, using evidence to figure out what’s implied but not directly stated.

6. **Connecting to Self, Text, and World** – Learners relate what they’ve read to their own experiences, other texts, or broader issues.

7. **Reflection Journals** – Learners write a short reflection on what they understood, found challenging, or found interesting.

8. **Graphic Organizers** – Concept maps, Venn diagrams, or T-charts to organize information from the text.
