# "I wish"...

Okay, let's break down the grammar of "I wish..." constructions in English, focusing on form, meaning, and use, and addressing common pitfalls for learners.

**1. Core Meaning and Purpose: Expressing Desire for Unreality**

The phrase "I wish..." expresses a desire for a situation that is different from the present reality, or a regret about a past situation that cannot be changed. It conveys a sense of longing, unfulfillment, or even mild frustration. It's inherently hypothetical and counterfactual (contrary to fact).

**2. Form: The Basic Structure**

The basic structure is:

**"I wish" + [Subject] + [Verb in Past Tense Form]**

The crucial point here is the shift in verb tense *after* "I wish." Even though we're often talking about the present or future, we use past tense forms to indicate the unreal or hypothetical nature of the wish. This is the key to understanding the grammar.

**3. Specific Forms and Meanings by Tense:**

Let's break this down by the tense of the verb *following* "I wish," and what it signifies.

- **3. 1. I Wish + Past Simple (for Present/Future Unreal Situations)**

    - **Form:** "I wish" + [Subject] + [Past Simple Verb]

        - Example: "I wish I *had* a million dollars." (I don't have a million dollars.)

        - Example: "I wish she *were* here." (She is not here.)

            - **Note:** With the verb "to be," "were" is traditionally used for all subjects in this subjunctive mood ("I wish I were," "I wish you were," "I wish he/she/it were," "I wish we were," "I wish they were"). While "was" is increasingly common in informal speech ("I wish I was..."), "were" is generally preferred in formal writing and is considered grammatically more correct.

    - **Meaning:** This expresses a desire for a different present or future situation. It implies that the current situation is *not* what you want.

    - **Use:** This is the most common form of "I wish." You use it for things you want to be true now or in the future, but aren't.

        - Example: "I wish I *knew* the answer." (Present – I don't know the answer.)

        - Example: "I wish it *rained* tomorrow." (Future – It probably won't rain, but I want it to.)

        - Example: "I wish I *spoke* French." (I don't speak French).

- **3.2. I Wish + Past Perfect (for Regrets About the Past)**

    - **Form:** "I wish" + [Subject] + [Past Perfect Verb (had + past participle)]

        - Example: "I wish I *had studied* harder for the exam." (I didn't study hard, and now I regret it.)

        - Example: "I wish she *hadn't said* that." (She said it, and I regret that she did.)

    - **Meaning:** This expresses regret about something that happened (or didn't happen) in the past. You cannot change the past, so this wish is inherently unfulfilled.

    - **Use:** This is used to express regret about past actions or inactions.

        - Example: "I wish I *had gone* to the party." (I didn't go, and I regret it.)

        - Example: "I wish they *had told* me earlier." (They didn't tell me earlier, and I'm unhappy about that.)

        - Example: "I wish I *hadn't eaten* so much cake." (I ate too much cake).

- **3.3. I Wish + Would + Base Verb (for Frustration/Desire for Change in Someone Else's Behavior or a Situation You Can't Control)**

    - **Form:** "I wish" + [Subject] + "would" + [Base Verb]

        - Example: "I wish he *would stop* talking." (He's talking, and I want him to stop.)

        - Example: "I wish the rain *would stop*." (It's raining, and I want it to stop.)

    - **Meaning:** This expresses a desire for a change in someone else's behavior or in a situation that is generally outside your control. It often carries a sense of frustration or annoyance. It's important to note that you *cannot* use this form to talk about your *own* behavior.

    - **Use:** This is used to express a desire for someone else to do (or not do) something, or for a situation to change.

        - Example: "I wish my neighbor *would turn down* the music." (Annoyance at the neighbor's behavior.)

        - Example: "I wish the bus *would arrive*." (Frustration at the bus being late.)

        - Example: "I wish people *would be* kinder to each other."

    - **Incorrect Usage:** *"I wish I would stop smoking."* This is incorrect. You should use the past simple: "I wish I *stopped* smoking" (or "I wish I *could* stop smoking"). Because you have control over your own actions, "would" is inappropriate.

- **3.4. I Wish + Could + Base Verb (for Ability/Possibility)**

    - **Form:** "I wish" + [Subject] + "could" + [Base Verb]

        - Example: "I wish I *could* fly."

        - Example: "I wish I *could* speak Japanese."

    - **Meaning:** This expresses a desire for an ability or possibility that you do not currently possess. It is closely related to the past simple form, but emphasizes the lack of ability.

    - **Use:** It is used for wishes about abilities or possibilities.

        - Example: "I wish I *could* come to the party, but I have to work." (Indicates an inability to attend.)

        - Example: "I wish we *could* afford a new car."

**4. Common Misconceptions and Errors for Learners:**

- **Using Present Tense After "I Wish":** This is the most frequent mistake. Learners often say things like *"I wish I have a car"* instead of "I wish I *had* a car." Emphasize the backshift in tense to indicate the unreal nature of the wish.

- **Using "Will" Instead of "Would":** Learners might say *"I wish he will stop talking"* instead of "I wish he *would* stop talking." Remind them that "would" is the correct modal verb to express a desire for a change in someone else's behavior.

- **Confusing "Would" and "Could":** "Would" is for a desired action, while "could" is for a desired ability.

- **Using "Would" for Self-Directed Wishes:** As mentioned earlier, you cannot use "would" to express a wish about your own behavior.

- **Forgetting the Past Perfect for Past Regrets:** Learners might say *"I wish I went to the party"* instead of "I wish I *had gone* to the party." The past perfect is crucial to indicate that the action is in the past and cannot be changed.

- **Using "to" before the base verb:** Avoid adding "to" between the auxiliary "would," or "could" and the base verb.

- **Incorrect:** "I wish I could to speak Japanese."

- **Correct:** "I wish I could speak Japanese."

**5. Teaching Strategies:**

- **Contrast Reality and Wish:** Use clear examples that contrast the current reality with the desired situation. For example, show a picture of someone working and say, "He's at work. He wishes he were on vacation."

- **Role-Playing:** Have students practice expressing wishes in different scenarios.

- **Error Correction:** Focus on correcting the tense errors gently but consistently.

- **Visual Aids:** Use timelines to illustrate the difference between past regrets (past perfect) and present/future wishes (past simple).

- **Contextualized Practice:** Create exercises that provide realistic contexts for using "I wish..."

By understanding the form, meaning, and use of these different "I wish..." structures, and by addressing the common errors, English language learners can master this important grammatical concept and express their desires and regrets accurately and effectively.
