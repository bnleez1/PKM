# VF - verb phrase formation is incorrect

A **Verb Phrase Formation (VF)** error occurs when a writer selects the correct action and tense for a sentence, but mechanically constructs the combination of helping verbs and main verbs incorrectly.

---

# The Grammar Formula: Understanding the "VF" Code

In English, verbs are rarely just single words. To express complex timeframes, ongoing actions, or passive ideas, English builds "verb phrases"—a team of words working together to create a single action.

When your teacher marks a **VF** in the margins, it is actually a compliment to your comprehension. It means the reader knows exactly *when* the action is happening and *what* you want to say. The error is simply that the mechanical "recipe" of the verb phrase is missing an ingredient or using the wrong form.

Here is a look at how verb phrases break down and how you can fix them.

## The 3 Most Common "VF" Traps

Verb phrases are built using a **main verb** (the action) and one or more **helping verbs** (like *is, are, have, has, do, did, will*). A VF error usually happens when these two parts fight each other.

### 1. The Missing Helper

English relies heavily on helping verbs to show that an action is ongoing. In many other languages, you only need the main verb to show this.

- **The Error:** "The students *working* on the project right now."

- **The Reality:** The "-ing" ending cannot work alone as a verb. It always needs a form of the "be" verb to help it.

- **The Fix:** "The students **are** *working* on the project right now."

### 2. The Double Past Tense

When you ask a question or make a negative statement in the past tense, English uses the helper verb *did*. The trap here is trying to make both the helping verb AND the main verb past tense.

- **The Error:** "The government *didn't passed* the new law."

- **The Reality:** In English, the helping verb carries the "weight" of the time. Because *did* is already in the past tense, the main verb gets to relax in its base form.

- **The Fix:** "The government *didn't* ***pass*** the new law."

### 3. The Passive or Perfect Mix-Up

Advanced English relies on the "Past Participle" (the third column in a verb chart, like *eaten, written, gone*). A common VF mistake is using the simple past tense word instead of the past participle when building a perfect tense or passive voice sentence.

- **The Error:** "The essay was *wrote* by my partner."

- **The Reality:** Passive voice requires the "be" verb plus the past participle, not the simple past.

- **The Fix:** "The essay was ***written*** by my partner."

---

## How to Self-Correct "VF" Errors

The best way to fix Verb Phrase Formation errors is to stop treating grammar like an art, and start treating it like a math formula.

When you review your rough draft, look at the complex verbs and check them against their required formulas:

| Structure         | The Formula                   | The Error         | The Fix               |
| :---------------- | :---------------------------- | :---------------- | :-------------------- |
| **Continuous**    | 'Be' verb + verb-ing          | They are play.    | They **are playing**. |
| **Perfect**       | 'Have' verb + past participle | She has see it.   | She **has seen** it.  |
| **Negative Past** | 'Did not' + base verb         | He didn't walked. | He **didn't walk**.   |
| **Passive Voice** | 'Be' verb + past participle   | It was build.     | It **was built**.     |

Getting a "VF" error means you are taking risks with your writing. You are moving away from simple subject-verb sentences and attempting complex, academic timeframes. Keep the formulas nearby as you edit, and those structures will soon become second nature.
