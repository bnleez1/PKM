# Gerunds and possessive forms before gerunds

### **Understanding the TOEFL Grammar Item: Gerunds and Possessive Forms Before Gerunds**

The given sentence is:

**"Tom's parents did not approve of _____ in a rock band."**

Answer choices:

1. **him joining**

2. **he joining**

3. **his join**

4. **he join**

To determine the correct answer, we need to analyze the grammatical structure of the phrase **"approve of"**, understand gerund usage, and address common misunderstandings.

---

### **1. Identifying the Grammar Rule: Gerunds and Their Subjects**

The key to this question is recognizing that the verb **"approve of"** is followed by a **gerund (-ing form)** because prepositions (like **"of"**) must be followed by either a noun or a gerund.

- **Correct structure:**

    **approve of + gerund (verb + -ing)**

    **approve of joining**

However, when a **gerund** has a subject (the person doing the action), English allows for **two possible structures**:

1. **Possessive before the gerund** (formal, more grammatically correct)

    - ✅ **approve of his joining**

2. **Object pronoun before the gerund** (more common in speech and informal writing)

    - ✅ **approve of him joining**

Thus, the correct answer is:

✅ **(A) him joining.**

*(Although “his joining” would also be correct in formal writing, it is not given as an answer choice.)*

---

### **2. Explanation of Why Other Choices Are Incorrect**

1. **(B) he joining** ❌

    - "He" is a subject pronoun (**he, she, they**), but it is not used before gerunds in this context.

    - Correct alternatives are either a **possessive pronoun** (his, her, their) or an **object pronoun** (him, her, them).

    - ❌ *approve of he joining* (incorrect)

    - ✅ *approve of him joining* (correct)

2. **(C) his join** ❌

    - "Join" is a **base verb**, but after "approve of," we need a **gerund (joining)** instead.

    - Also, "his join" is not a natural phrase in English.

    - ❌ *approve of his join* (incorrect)

    - ✅ *approve of his joining* (correct, but not in answer choices)

3. **(D) he join** ❌

    - "Join" in this form would be a **bare infinitive** (used in commands or subjunctive clauses), but it is incorrect after "approve of."

    - ❌ *approve of he join* (incorrect)

    - ✅ *approve of him joining* (correct)

---

### **3. Common Misunderstandings and Errors**

Many English learners struggle with this type of sentence because of confusion over **possessive pronouns before gerunds, subject vs. object pronouns, and gerund usage after prepositions.**

#### **A. Confusion Between "him joining" and "his joining"**

- Both **"him joining"** and **"his joining"** can be grammatically correct, but **"him joining"** is more commonly used in everyday speech.

- "His joining" sounds more formal and academic.

- **Both are correct:**

    - ✅ *approve of his joining* (formal)

    - ✅ *approve of him joining* (common)

#### **B. Thinking "He" Works as the Subject**

- Many learners mistakenly believe **"he joining"** is correct because "he" is a subject pronoun. However, before a gerund, we use **a possessive pronoun or an object pronoun**, not a subject pronoun.

- ❌ *approve of he joining* (incorrect)

- ✅ *approve of him joining* (correct)

#### **C. Forgetting That Prepositions Require a Gerund**

- Since "approve of" is followed by a **preposition**, the next verb must be in **gerund form (-ing).**

- Many learners mistakenly try to use the **base verb (join)** instead of **joining.**

- ❌ *approve of his join* (incorrect)

- ❌ *approve of he join* (incorrect)

- ✅ *approve of him joining* (correct)

---

### **4. Summary and Final Takeaway**

- **Rule**: After **"approve of"**, use a **gerund (-ing form)**.

- **Correct Answer**: *him joining* (Answer A).

- **Common Mistakes**:

    1. Using **"he joining"** instead of **"him joining."**

    2. Using **"join"** instead of **"joining."**

    3. Thinking **"his join"** makes sense (it does not).

    4. Forgetting that **prepositions must be followed by a noun or gerund**.

To master this structure, learners should practice sentences with **prepositions + gerunds**, such as:

- **They insisted on him attending the meeting.**

- **She is worried about her failing the test.**

- **We talked about them moving to Canada.**

By understanding these patterns, English learners can avoid this common TOEFL grammar mistake and improve their accuracy.
