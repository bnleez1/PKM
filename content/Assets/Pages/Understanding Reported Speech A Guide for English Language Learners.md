# Understanding Reported Speech: A Guide for English Language Learners

### Introduction

Reported speech, also known as indirect speech, is a way to convey what someone else has said without quoting their exact words. This is an essential skill for English language learners as it helps in summarizing conversations, reporting news, and sharing information accurately. In this article, we will explore how to form reported speech and its practical applications in everyday communication.

### What is Reported Speech?

Reported speech involves relaying what someone else has said without using their exact words. For example, if John says, “I am going to the store,” you can report this by saying, “John said that he was going to the store.” Notice how the verb tense changes and the pronoun shifts to match the context of the reporting.

### Key Changes in Reported Speech

When converting direct speech to reported speech, several changes typically occur:

1. **Pronoun Changes**: Pronouns often change to match the perspective of the reporter.

    - Direct: “I am happy,” she said.

    - Reported: She said that she was happy.

2. **Tense Changes**: The tense usually shifts back one step in time.

    - Present Simple to Past Simple: “I like ice cream,” he said. → He said that he liked ice cream.

    - Present Continuous to Past Continuous: “I am reading a book,” she said. → She said that she was reading a book.

    - Present Perfect to Past Perfect: “I have finished my homework,” he said. → He said that he had finished his homework.

3. **Time and Place Changes**: Words indicating time and place may need to be adjusted.

    - “Today” becomes “that day.”

    - “Tomorrow” becomes “the next day.”

    - “Here” becomes “there.”

### Forming Reported Speech

To form reported speech, follow these steps:

1. **Identify the Reporting Verb**: Common reporting verbs include “say,” “tell,” “ask,” “explain,” and “mention.”

    - Example: “She said,” “He told me,” “They asked.”

2. **Change the Pronouns**: Adjust the pronouns to fit the context of the report.

    - Example: “I” becomes “he” or “she,” “we” becomes “they.”

3. **Shift the Tense**: Move the tense back one step in time.

    - Example: Present Simple to Past Simple, Present Continuous to Past Continuous.

4. **Adjust Time and Place Words**: Modify words that indicate time and place.

    - Example: “Now” becomes “then,” “here” becomes “there.”

### Examples of Reported Speech

Let’s look at some examples to illustrate these changes:

1. **Direct Speech**: “I will call you tomorrow,” she said.

    - **Reported Speech**: She said that she would call me the next day.

2. **Direct Speech**: “We are meeting at the park,” they said.

    - **Reported Speech**: They said that they were meeting at the park.

3. **Direct Speech**: “I have never been to Paris,” he said.

    - **Reported Speech**: He said that he had never been to Paris.

### Special Cases in Reported Speech

There are some special cases and exceptions to the general rules of reported speech:

1. **Modal Verbs**: Some modal verbs change in reported speech.

    - “Can” becomes “could.”

    - “Will” becomes “would.”

    - “May” becomes “might.”

2. **Questions**: When reporting questions, the sentence structure changes.

    - Direct: “Where are you going?” she asked.

    - Reported: She asked where I was going.

3. **Commands and Requests**: Use “tell” for commands and “ask” for requests.

    - Direct: “Please open the window,” he said.

    - Reported: He asked me to open the window.

    - Direct: “Sit down,” she said.

    - Reported: She told me to sit down.

### Practical Applications of Reported Speech

Understanding and using reported speech is crucial in various real-world contexts:

1. **Journalism**: Reporters often use reported speech to convey statements made by public figures.

    - Example: The president said that the new policy would create jobs.

2. **Everyday Conversations**: Sharing information from one person to another.

    - Example: “Mom said that dinner will be ready at 7 PM.”

3. **Academic Writing**: Summarizing research findings or citing sources.

    - Example: The study concluded that exercise improves mental health.

4. **Professional Settings**: Reporting meetings or discussions.

    - Example: The manager mentioned that the project deadline is next Friday.

### Practice Exercises

To master reported speech, practice is essential. Here are some exercises to help you:

1. **Convert Direct to Reported Speech**:

    - Direct: “I am learning English,” she said.

    - Reported: She said that she was learning English.

2. **Identify Changes**:

    - Direct: “We will travel to Japan next year,” they said.

    - Reported: They said that they would travel to Japan the following year.

    - Identify the changes in pronouns, tense, and time words.

3. **Report Questions**:

    - Direct: “What time does the train leave?” he asked.

    - Reported: He asked what time the train left.

4. **Report Commands and Requests**:

    - Direct: “Please turn off the lights,” she said.

    - Reported: She asked me to turn off the lights.

### Conclusion

Reported speech is a fundamental aspect of English that allows you to convey what others have said accurately and effectively. By understanding the rules and practicing regularly, you can improve your ability to use reported speech in various contexts. Remember to pay attention to pronoun changes, tense shifts, and adjustments in time and place words. With these skills, you’ll be able to communicate more clearly and confidently in English.

## Examples in each verb tense

1. **Present Simple**

    - **Direct Speech**: “I eat breakfast at 7 AM,” she said.

    - **Reported Speech**: She said that she ate breakfast at 7 AM.

2. **Present Continuous**

    - **Direct Speech**: “I am reading a book,” he said.

    - **Reported Speech**: He said that he was reading a book.

3. **Present Perfect**

    - **Direct Speech**: “I have finished my homework,” she said.

    - **Reported Speech**: She said that she had finished her homework.

4. **Present Perfect Continuous**

    - **Direct Speech**: “I have been working here for five years,” he said.

    - **Reported Speech**: He said that he had been working there for five years.

5. **Past Simple**

    - **Direct Speech**: “I visited Paris last year,” she said.

    - **Reported Speech**: She said that she had visited Paris the previous year.

6. **Past Continuous**

    - **Direct Speech**: “I was watching TV when you called,” he said.

    - **Reported Speech**: He said that he had been watching TV when I called.

7. **Past Perfect**

    - **Direct Speech**: “I had already eaten when they arrived,” she said.

    - **Reported Speech (doesn’t change)**: She said that she had already eaten when they arrived.

8. **Past Perfect Continuous**

    - **Direct Speech**: “I had been studying for two hours when you came,” he said.

    - **Reported Speech (doesn’t change)**: He said that he had been studying for two hours when I came.

9. **Future Simple**

    - **Direct Speech**: “I will call you tomorrow,” she said.

    - **Reported Speech**: She said that she would call me the next day.

10. **Future Continuous**
*   **Direct Speech**: “I will be traveling to London next week,” he said.
*   **Reported Speech**: He said that he would be traveling to London the following week.

11. **Future Perfect**
*   **Direct Speech**: “I will have finished the project by Monday,” she said.
*   **Reported Speech**: She said that she would have finished the project by Monday.

12. **Future Perfect Continuous**
*   **Direct Speech**: “I will have been working here for ten years next month,” he said.
*   **Reported Speech**: He said that he would have been working there for ten years next month.
