# W-Chapter 11: Improving Coherence and Word Choice


⚡️ Using critical feedback to revise an expository or descriptive draft

> Clear writing depends on more than correct sentences. Readers also need a logical path from the topic sentence through the supporting details to the closing sentence. This quality is coherence. They also need vocabulary that expresses the writer’s meaning accurately. This week, you will examine your own expository or descriptive draft for problems with clarity, connectors, lexical choice, and punctuation. With authorization, GenAI may identify possible areas for improvement or present brief alternatives for individual words, connectors, or structures. It may not replace your paragraph. Every suggestion must be checked against dictionaries, course materials, model texts, or teacher guidance. The final choice belongs to you, and every change should be understandable and explainable. Critical verification protects both accuracy and your authentic voice.


## Essential understanding(s)


- How can a writer make the logic of a paragraph visible to the reader?

- How can language alternatives be evaluated critically rather than accepted automatically?

## Guiding Questions

- Where does a paragraph lose focus or move too quickly between ideas?

- How can connectors, repeated key words, and logical order support coherence?

- What makes a vocabulary alternative appropriate for meaning and A2 level?

- Which sources can verify an AI or peer suggestion?

## Learning outcomes

- Diagnose problems with focus, order, connectors, lexical choice, and punctuation.

- Revise an expository or descriptive draft for coherence and cohesion.

- Compare brief language alternatives and select appropriate A2 options.

- Verify suggestions with dictionaries, course materials, models, or teacher input.

- Explain why each significant change preserves or improves intended meaning.

## Main chapter content

### Diagnosing coherence problems

Read only the topic sentence and the first few words of each supporting sentence. Can you predict the paragraph’s direction? If a sentence introduces a new topic, decide whether it should be removed, connected more clearly, or developed in another paragraph. Next, summarize each sentence in two or three words. The sequence of summaries should form a logical path. If it does not, reorganize the ideas before changing vocabulary.

### Using cohesive signals

Coherence comes from overall logic; cohesion comes from language connections. Connectors show relationships, but repeated key words and clear references also matter. A paragraph about a future goal might repeat goal, practice, and progress while using pronouns carefully. Do not rely on connectors to hide weak organization. “In addition” cannot make an unrelated sentence relevant. First decide how the idea supports the focus, then choose a connector if the relationship needs to be explicit.

### Evaluating word alternatives

A suggested synonym may change meaning, formality, or grammar. For example, trip, travel, and journey are related but not always interchangeable. Check the dictionary definition, word class, and example sentence. Also ask whether the alternative is appropriate for A2 and whether you can use it in your own sentence. A familiar precise word is often better than a sophisticated word that does not sound like you or requires a grammar pattern you have not learned.

### Using GenAI as a limited diagnostic tool

After completing your own draft, an authorized prompt may ask: “Point out two places where the relationship between sentences is unclear. Do not rewrite the paragraph.” Another may ask for three A2 alternatives to one repeated word. Treat the response as a list of possibilities, not a correction key. Verify each suggestion. The tool may overcorrect, introduce advanced language, or misunderstand a culturally specific detail. Record the prompt, response, decision, and source used for verification.

### Using a four-question verification routine

When you receive a suggested word or structure, apply four questions. Meaning: Does it express the idea I intend? Grammar: Does it fit the sentence pattern and word class? Level: Is it appropriate for A2 and understandable to me? Voice: Does it sound like a choice I can explain and use again? Then consult a reliable source. A bilingual dictionary may confirm basic meaning, while an English learner dictionary can show example patterns and common combinations. A model text can show whether a connector fits the paragraph position. The teacher can clarify differences that a dictionary does not resolve. Record only the verification needed for important changes; the purpose is not to create excessive paperwork. Over time, this routine develops independence because you learn to treat every suggestion as a hypothesis that requires evidence rather than as an instruction that must be followed.

### Revising with explainable decisions

Make changes in stages. First, revise focus and order. Second, improve cohesion through key words, references, and connectors. Third, examine lexical precision. Finally, edit punctuation. For each significant change, write a brief explanation: “I moved the example after the reason because the reader needs the general point first,” or “I kept train instead of transportation system because the paragraph refers to one specific service.” The ability to explain a change is evidence that you remain the author.
