# Discourse Analysis Unit III: Meaning in Interaction

## 🎯 Stage 1 — Desired Results

### A. Established Goals

Students will deconstruct authentic spoken interactions to comprehend the pragmatic functions of utterances and the collaborative nature of human conversation.

### B. Transfer Goals

Students will independently use their learning to:

- Become "Learning Weavers" who transparently design and share communicative classroom activities that mirror the authentic, unstructured nature of real-world interaction.

- Diagnose conversational breakdowns in the language classroom by evaluating the pragmatic failures and unspoken rules of human turn-taking, rather than just grammatical errors.

### C. Meaning

**Enduring Understandings (Students will understand that …)**

- Utterances perform actions (locutionary, illocutionary, and perlocutionary acts) rather than merely transferring information.

- Human conversation is a highly structured, collaborative process governed by unspoken rules, including adjacency pairs and turn-taking mechanics.

- There is a distinct functional difference between interactional (social) and transactional (informative) talk, and both are necessary for communicative competence.

**Essential Questions**

- How do we use language to "do" things in the real world?

- Why do conversations sometimes break down even when the vocabulary and grammar are perfectly correct?

- How can we make the invisible, collaborative rules of social interaction visible and teachable to English language learners?

### D. Acquisition

**Students will know …**

- The fundamentals of Speech Act Theory (locutionary, illocutionary, and perlocutionary acts).

- The mechanics of adjacency pairs and the rules governing turn-taking.

- The defining characteristics of interactional (social) versus transactional (informative) talk.

**Students will be able to …**

- Transcribe short, publicly available audio or video clips of natural conversations.

- Code conversational transcripts to label speech acts, turn-taking signals, and adjacency pairs.

- Evaluate breakdowns in conversational flow and identify pragmatic failures.

- Design role-play activities for the ELT classroom that highlight interactional vs. transactional talk.

## 🎯 Stage 2 — Evidence

### A. Performance Task (GRASPS)

- **Goal:** Demystify spoken pragmatics by transcribing a natural conversation, coding its hidden mechanics, and designing a classroom-ready role-play activity that brings this theory into practice.

- **Role:** An ELT educator who models transparent learning and teaching.

- **Audience:** Pre-service teachers, language learners, and your broader Personal Learning Network (PLN) of educators.

- **Situation:** To contribute to an engaging, open community of educators, you are preparing a multi-modal instructional breakdown. You will select a short, publicly available audio or video clip of a natural conversation and transcribe it. You will then code the transcript for speech acts, turn-taking, and adjacency pairs. Finally, you will use this analysis to design a practical role-play activity highlighting interactional versus transactional talk for the ELT classroom.

- **Product/Performance:** A "Pragmatics in Action" resource package. This could be formatted as an ELT podcast episode script, a Substack newsletter post containing the annotated transcript, or a video breakdown. The product must incorporate theoretical grounding from Cook (1989) Section 1, McCarthy (1991) Chapter 5, and Searle (1969).

**Standards/Rubric (summary):** (Note: Unit III assignments and quizzes represent 30% of the total course grade.)

- **Purpose & audience:** The breakdown transparently connects theoretical linguistics with practical classroom application for a public audience of peers.

- **Organization & cohesion:** The transcript is accurately coded, and the resulting role-play activity logically aligns with the identified interactional or transactional features.

- **Detail & diction:** Speech Act Theory fundamentals (locutionary, illocutionary, perlocutionary) are applied accurately to evaluate the interaction.

- **Language control (B1+):** Professional, engaging register is maintained; the use of English accounts for 50% of the evaluation.

- **Process & integrity:** APA style is utilized for all citations (including the media source); the work is 100% original, complying strictly with departmental AI and plagiarism policies.

**B. Other Evidence**

- **Formative Quizzes:** Knowledge checks covering the core concepts of Searle’s Speech Acts and the mechanics of adjacency pairs.

- **Pragmatic Failure Audit:** A short evaluation of a provided conversational breakdown transcript to identify where and why the communication failed pragmatically.

- **Transcription Workshop:** Collaborative, in-class practice transcribing and coding short audio samples to build practical skills before the final task.
