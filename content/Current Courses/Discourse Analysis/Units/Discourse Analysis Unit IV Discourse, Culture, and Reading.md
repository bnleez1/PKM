# Discourse Analysis Unit IV: Discourse, Culture, and Reading

## 🎯 Stage 1 — Desired Results

### A. Established Goals

- Students will critically evaluate written texts to uncover cultural rhetorical patterns.

- Students will analyze the relationship between macro-discourse structures and reading comprehension.

### B. Transfer Goals

Students will independently use their learning to:

- Demystify the reading process for their learners by making macro-discourse structures and cultural writing patterns explicitly visible in the ELT classroom.

- Share transparent, culturally responsive lesson frameworks with the wider public, contributing to an engaging and inclusive global community of educators.

### C. Meaning

**Enduring Understandings (Students will understand that …)**

- Processing speech and processing writing require fundamentally different cognitive and structural approaches.

- Writing is not culturally neutral; cultural norms dictate logical flow, paragraph structure, and rhetorical patterns (Contrastive Rhetoric).

- Top-down and bottom-up reading processes rely heavily on an understanding of macro-discourse structures to achieve full comprehension.

**Essential Questions**

- How does our cultural background silently dictate the way we organize our thoughts on paper?

- Why do English language learners often struggle with reading comprehension even when they know all the vocabulary?

- How can educators design transparent reading activities that bridge the gap between different cultural rhetorical patterns?

### D. Acquisition

**Students will know …**

- The cognitive and structural differences between processing speech and writing.

- The concept of Contrastive Rhetoric and how culture influences writing patterns.

- The relationship between discourse structure and top-down/bottom-up reading processes.

**Students will be able to …**

- Compare texts across different cultural contexts to identify distinct rhetorical patterns.

- Deconstruct written texts to analyze how cultural norms dictate logical flow and paragraph structure.

- Design pre-reading and while-reading activities that leverage macro-discourse structures to aid ELT learner comprehension.

## 🎯 Stage 2 — Evidence

### A. Performance Task (GRASPS)

- **Goal:** Create a "Cultural Rhetoric Reading Workshop" that deconstructs written texts from different cultural contexts and provides actionable reading comprehension activities based on macro-discourse structures.

- **Role:** An ELT teacher trainer committed to transparent pedagogical practices.

- **Audience:** The public at large, specifically targeting pre-service teachers and ELT colleagues looking to make their reading lessons more culturally responsive.

- **Situation:** To empower your community of educators, you are publishing a comparative analysis. You will select two written texts from different cultural contexts and deconstruct how their cultural norms dictate logical flow. Using this analysis, you will design pre-reading and while-reading activities that leverage these macro-discourse structures to aid learner comprehension.

- **Product/Performance:** A publicly shareable resource package (e.g., a digital workshop recorded via screencast, a comprehensive teaching blog post, or an interactive OER module). The project must be theoretically grounded using McCarthy (1991) Chapter 6 and Celce-Murcia & Olshtan (2006).

**Standards/Rubric (summary):** (Note: Unit IV assignments and quizzes represent 30% of the total course grade.)

- **Purpose & audience:** The workshop transparently connects cultural rhetorical theory to practical ELT reading strategies in a way that is highly accessible to the public.

- **Organization & cohesion:** The text deconstruction logically highlights differences in rhetorical patterns, and the designed activities clearly support top-down/bottom-up reading processes.

- **Detail & diction:** Accurately applies concepts of Contrastive Rhetoric and cognitive processing.

- **Language control (B1+):** High grammatical accuracy and appropriate academic/instructional register; the use of English accounts for 50% of the item's evaluated value.

- **Process & integrity:** Adheres strictly to APA style for written portions and citations; complies fully with departmental policies on plagiarism and AI use, resulting in a zero (0%) if violated.

**B. Other Evidence**

- **Formative Quizzes:** Assessing conceptual knowledge of Contrastive Rhetoric and the differences between processing speech and writing.

- **Cultural Text Comparison Log:** A collaborative digital log where students compare short texts across cultures to identify distinct rhetorical patterns before applying them to their final project.

- **Activity Design Drafts:** In-class drafts of pre-reading and while-reading activities leveraging macro-discourse structures, subjected to peer review.
