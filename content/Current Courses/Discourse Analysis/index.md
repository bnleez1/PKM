# Course Description

This theoretical and practical subject focuses on analyzing the relationship between language and the diverse contexts in which it is used—both in written and oral forms—from various existing approaches. It promotes the analysis of the meaning and structure of communicative acts while considering their context from the perspective of teaching English as a foreign language. Through its content and learning experiences, this course contributes to the objectives of the Institutional Humanistic Training Program and its partial accreditation. The course precedes Introduction to Linguistics and Morphology, English Phonology and Phonetics, Sociolinguistics, Psycholinguistics, Spanish Grammar, English Grammar, and English Literature I and II; is taken concurrently with Group Practice; and is an antecedent to Applied Linguistics, Teaching Practice, Professional Practicum I and II, and the Integration Seminar.
# Course Objective

Upon completion of the semester, the student will analyze oral and written texts in the English language from different approaches to discourse analysis. This will serve as a foundation to support the teaching of English in a pluralistic, high-quality manner, maintaining an openness to diverse cultural, social, and ideological contexts.
