# Discourse Analysis Module 08: The Architecture of Conversation
[[DA Module 08 Note]]

⚡ **Adjacency pairs, turn-taking, and conversational coding**

Conversation is organized through recognizable patterns. A question normally expects an answer, a greeting expects a greeting, and a request creates a space for acceptance or refusal. Speakers also coordinate when to speak, when to continue, and when to give another person the floor. This week, learners examine these patterns and code short transcripts for **speech acts, adjacency pairs, and turn-taking signals**.

## Essential Understandings

1. How do speakers organize conversation turn by turn?
2. How do adjacency pairs and turn-taking help participants understand what should happen next?

## Guiding Questions

1. How do first-pair parts create expectations for second-pair parts?
2. How do speakers know when a turn may be complete?
3. What signals help speakers take, hold, or give up the floor?
4. How can a transcript be coded for speech acts, adjacency pairs, and turn-taking?
## Learning Outcomes

By the end of the week, learners will be able to:

- Identify common adjacency pairs in conversational transcripts.
- Distinguish first-pair parts from second-pair parts.
- Identify turn-taking signals and possible transition-relevance places.
- Recognize pauses, overlap, and speaker selection.
- Code a short transcript for **speech acts, adjacency pairs, and turn-taking features**.
- Support coding decisions with evidence from the conversation.

## Key Vocabulary

|Term|Simple definition|
|---|---|
|**Adjacency pair**|Two related conversational actions, such as question–answer.|
|**First-pair part (FPP)**|The first action that makes a response relevant.|
|**Second-pair part (SPP)**|The expected response to the first action.|
|**Conditional relevance**|The expectation that one action makes another action appropriate next.|
|**Turn**|One speaker's contribution to a conversation.|
|**Turn-taking**|How speakers organize who speaks next.|
|**Transition-relevance place (TRP)**|A possible point where another speaker may begin.|
|**Overlap**|Two speakers talking at the same time.|
|**Pause**|Silence within or between turns.|
|**Repair**|Fixing a problem in speaking, hearing, or understanding.|
