# Discourse Analysis Module 11: Speech Acts, Turn-Taking, and Role-Play Design

> **Module Overview**  
> This module transitions students from macro-level conversational structures into micro-pragmatic coding and instructional design. Students evaluate authentic vs. synthetic spoken discourse, investigate speech acts and conversational mechanics, and bridge discourse findings into feasible ELT role-play tasks.

---

## 🧭 Unit III Context & Summary: The Dynamics of Spoken Interaction

### Theoretical Framework & Scope
**Unit III (Spoken Interaction)** shifts analytical focus from static, written text to real-time, interactive spoken communication. Unlike written discourse—which often emphasizes planned thematic progression and explicit cohesion—spoken discourse is collaborative, fleeting, and highly context-dependent. 

Key pillars covered across Unit III include:
1. **Pragmatics & Action Theory:** 
   - **Austin & Searle's Speech Act Theory:** Analyzing utterances not merely as grammatical sentences, but as functional actions classified into **locutionary** (literal form), **illocutionary** (communicative intent/force), and **perlocutionary** (consequential effect on the hearer) acts.
   - **Direct vs. Indirect Speech Acts:** How politeness, hedging, and social distance shape conversational choices.
2. **Conversational Analysis (CA) Mechanics:**
   - **Adjacency Pairs & Preference Organization:** Ordered two-part exchanges (e.g., greeting–greeting, question–answer, invitation–acceptance/refusal) and the structural asymmetry between preferred (unmarked) and dispreferred (marked, hesitant, justified) responses.
   - **Turn-Taking System:** The management of Transition Relevance Places (TRPs), interruptions, overlaps, pause management, and backchannel signaling.
   - **Transactional vs. Interactional Talk:** Distinguishing message-oriented exchanges (e.g., ordering food, asking for directions) from relationship-maintaining exchanges (e.g., small talk, phatic communion).
3. **Pedagogical Translation to ELT:**
   - Deconstructing the unnatural, artificial nature of traditional ELT dialogue scripts.
   - Leveraging pragmatic awareness to build realistic listening comprehension tasks, authentic speaking rubrics, and inclusive, communicative classroom activities.

---

## 🎯 Module 11 Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, students will be able to:
- **Analyze** raw conversational transcripts using Speech Act Theory and Conversational Analysis conventions.
- **Critically audit** GenAI-generated dialogue to identify artificiality, dispreferred turn markers, or pragmatic drift.
- **Design** a Universal Design for Learning (UDL)-aligned role-play task for an English as a Foreign Language (EFL) classroom based on verified spoken interaction data.

## 🧩 Module Breakdown: The 4-Stage Learning Loop

### 1. Applied Linguistics (Conceptual)
* **Speech Acts in Context:** Exploring how speaker intention is coded and decoded. Understanding *indirect speech acts* (e.g., *"Can you pass the salt?"* functioning as a directive request rather than an ability inquiry).
* **The Turn-Taking Machinery:** Identifying how participants signal speaker transition, negotiate conversational floors, and deploy repair sequences when miscommunication occurs.

### 2. English Language Analysis (Procedural)
* **Corpus & Interaction Coding:** Annotating transcripts for:
  - Adjacency pairs and response preferences.
  - Discourse markers (`well`, `you know`, `anyway`).
  - Ellipsis and non-fluency features (fillers, false starts, hesitations) typical of naturally occurring spoken English.

### 3. Learning Methodologies (Pedagogical Practice)
* **Critiquing Coursebook Dialogue vs. Real Talk:** Why scripted textbook dialogues often hinder pragmatic fluency by presenting sterile, perfectly balanced exchanges devoid of real turn-taking dynamics.
* **Universal Design for Learning (UDL) Integration:** Designing interactive role-play tasks with multiple means of **engagement**, **representation**, and **action/expression** to support diverse proficiency levels.

### 4. Practice & GenAI Integration (Critical AI Literacy — Level 3/4)

> [!tip] AI Practice Protocol: The Transcript Audit
> 1. **Prompting:** Use a generative AI tool (e.g., ChatGPT, Claude) to produce a 12-to-15 turn spoken transcript between two speakers handling a high-stakes, indirect transactional interaction (e.g., negotiating a late assignment or disputing a billing charge).
> 2. **Critical Verification & Coding:** 
>    - Annotate every turn for illocutionary force.
>    - Flag where the AI produces overly explicit, unnatural, or grammatically hyper-correct dialogue that violates authentic spoken turn-taking conventions.
> 3. **Human Revision:** Manually edit and reconstruct the transcript to restore authentic pragmatic markers, conversational overlap, hesitations, and culturally appropriate face-saving strategies.
