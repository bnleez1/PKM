# Academic Writing Unit I

## 🎯 Stage 1 — Desired Results

### A. Established Goals

- Students will explore and analyze the core principles of academic integrity through varied perspectives.

- Students will recognize, examine, and revise the foundational elements of academic writing, including introductions and conclusions, allowing for diverse approaches to topic selection.

### B. Transfer Goals

Students will independently use their learning to:

- Navigate complex ethical landscapes in modern research by transparently managing academic integrity.

- Systematically break down broad educational subjects into focused, manageable, and structured inquiries.

- Communicate theoretical frameworks effectively to diverse academic audiences using standardized conventions.

### C. Meaning

**Enduring Understandings (Students will understand that …)**

- Academic integrity is a continuous ethical practice of attribution and transparency, rather than simply a mechanism to avoid punishment.

- A well-structured academic paper acts as a predictable roadmap that guides the reader logically from a broad problem to a specific, actionable thesis.

- Narrowing a research focus, such as isolating specific practices like academic writing across the curriculum, is essential for depth, clarity, and validity in educational research.

**Essential Questions**

- What distinguishes true academic synthesis from mere compilation or plagiarism?

- How does the rigid structure of an academic text actually free a writer to make a more powerful argument?

- How do we maintain and define academic integrity in an era where modern tools influence research practices?

- How does adapting a research focus—such as exploring academic writing across the curriculum—shape the subsequent methodology and literature needed?

### D. Acquisition

**Students will know …**

- The definitions and implications of academic dishonesty, cheating, plagiarism, and unauthorized collaboration.

- Contemporary issues at the intersection of AI and research, including specific case analyses like the Nuñez Arancibia Case.

- The foundational definitions of academic writing and how to differentiate it from other writing forms.

- The structural breakdown of an academic paper: Title page, Abstract, Literature review, Methodology, Results/discussion, Conclusion, and References.

- The necessary components of an introduction (problem and thesis statement) and a conclusion.

**Students will be able to …**

- Utilize strategies to narrow down a broad subject into a specific, researchable topic.

- Draft the required components of an academic introduction and conclusion.

- Apply APA format guidelines accurately to academic texts.

## 🎯 Stage 2 — Evidence

### A. Performance Task (GRASPS)

- **Goal:** Design a comprehensive, structured research proposal that actively narrows a broad educational theme into a specific investigation of academic writing across the curriculum.

- **Role:** Educational Researcher / Language Teacher Trainer.

- **Audience:** The ELT Academy review board and peer researchers.

- **Situation:** You are establishing the theoretical and structural foundation for a major research project. Before compiling your full literature review, you must pitch a structurally sound proposal that clearly defines the problem, states a thesis, and outlines your conceptual framework. You must demonstrate strict adherence to contemporary academic integrity standards, including the transparent disclosure of your research process.

- **Product/Performance:** A drafted research proposal that includes a Title page, Abstract, Introduction (with an explicitly stated problem and thesis statement), and a preliminary Conclusion, all formatted strictly in APA style. To align with UDL, the pre-writing process mapping out this proposal may be submitted as a visual organizer, audio-recorded brainstorm, or traditional outline.

**Standards/Rubric (summary):**

- **Purpose & audience:** The proposal clearly addresses the academic review board, effectively narrowing the topic to a specific researchable problem.

- **Organization & cohesion:** The structural components (Title Page, Abstract, Introduction, Conclusion) are logically sequenced according to academic norms, with clear connections between the problem statement and thesis.

- **Detail & diction:** Demonstrates an understanding of vocabulary and rhetorical strategies appropriate for academic discourse.

- **Language control (B2+):** Consistently maintains a B2+ CEFR level of linguistic complexity and accuracy.

- **Process & integrity:** Demonstrates 100% adherence to academic integrity principles, explicitly avoiding plagiarism and unauthorized collaboration. APA formatting guidelines are applied flawlessly throughout the text.

**B. Other Evidence**

- Formative peer-assessment and self-assessment checklists evaluating the clarity of thesis statements in draft introductions.

- Reflective responses (written, audio, or video annotation) analyzing the ethical implications of the Nuñez Arancibia case and its relevance to modern research tools.

- Short, low-stakes APA formatting exercises identifying and correcting citation errors.
