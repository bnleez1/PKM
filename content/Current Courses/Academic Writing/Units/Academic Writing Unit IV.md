# Academic Writing Unit IV

## 🎯 Stage 1 — Desired Results

### A. Established Goals

Students will synthesize their cumulative writing skills and curated research to develop a unified, cohesive Literature Review that adheres to professional academic standards.

### B. Transfer Goals

Students will independently use their learning to:

- Model the rigorous, ethical synthesis of literature to make the academic writing process transparent for their own language learners.

- Author comprehensive reviews that map out educational frameworks, sharing these resources publicly to foster an engaging community of educators.

### C. Meaning

**Enduring Understandings (Students will understand that …)**

- A literature review is a unified narrative that structurally integrates an introduction, body, and conclusion, rather than a disconnected series of source summaries.

- Adhering to strict scope and scale expectations—such as specific word counts for structural sections—ensures the research remains focused and professionally viable.

- Authentic academic synthesis relies on the seamless, ethical application of previously vetted literature.

**Essential Questions**

- How do the introduction, body, and conclusion function together structurally to create a coherent academic product?

- How can researchers seamlessly integrate multiple external sources without losing the unity of their own academic voice?

- In what ways do strict formatting constraints and word limits actually enhance the clarity of a synthesized argument?

### D. Acquisition

**Students will know …**

- The function of structural integration: how an introduction, body, and conclusion work together to build a unified review.

- The specific scope and scale expectations of the final product, specifically that of a 2500-word review, 2000 words must comprise the body.

**Students will be able to …**

- Draft a cohesive Literature Review that strictly meets established word count requirements.

- Seamlessly integrate a minimum of 10 valid, reliable, and unbiased references, utilizing the exact sources curated previously in the Unit III annotated bibliography.

- Comply strictly with APA formatting guidelines throughout the entirety of the final product.

## 🎯 Stage 2 — Evidence

### A. Performance Task (GRASPS)

- **Goal:** Synthesize cumulative writing skills to author a unified, cohesive 2500-word Literature Review.

- **Role:** Language Teacher Trainer and Educational Researcher.

- **Audience:** An engaging public community of educators, peers, and the ELT Academy.

- **Situation:** You are culminating your research journey. To model academic best practices for your learners and peers, you will make your writing process transparent, demonstrating how to structurally synthesize the vetted sources from your annotated bibliography into a comprehensive narrative. This review will serve as a foundational resource outlining the current state of knowledge in your chosen area of educational practice.

- **Product/Performance:** A 2500-word Literature Review, where exactly 2000 words comprise the body, seamlessly integrating the exact 10 sources curated in Unit III. Following UDL principles, the drafting process will involve flexible grouping, prewriting modalities (visual organizers, audio brainstorming), and iterative formative feedback via ICTs before the final submission. This task constitutes 50% of the overall course assessment.

**Standards/Rubric (summary):**

- **Purpose & audience:** The review successfully synthesizes the selected topic for the academic community, presenting a unified and coherent narrative.

- **Organization & cohesion:** The structural integration of the introduction, body, and conclusion functions together flawlessly. The review meets the specific 2500-word scope, with 2000 words dedicated to the body.

- **Detail & diction:** The exact 10 sources from Unit III are seamlessly integrated into the text.

- **Language control (B2+):** The use of English represents a 50% value of the grade on the evaluation, demonstrating consistent B2+ academic language proficiency.

- **Process & integrity:** The document strictly adheres to APA formatting. Must yield a 90%-100% originality score, demonstrating zero plagiarism, generative AI usage, or translation software, which would result in a zero grade. Furthermore, to receive credit, the assignment must be turned in on time.

**B. Other Evidence**

- Self and peer-assessments evaluating the structural integration between the introduction, body, and conclusion using accessible, modeled examples and rubrics.

- Prewriting process maps (visual organizers or traditional outlines) explicitly planning the seamless integration points for the 10 sources from Unit III.

- Formative, ongoing feedback loops utilizing Information and Communication Technologies (ICTs) to deliver written comments, audio memos, or video annotations on literature review drafts.
