# LISTENING MODULE 12: ESTABLISHING DREAMS & FUTURE GOALS
⚡️ *Autonomous Listening Across Personal Narratives, Itineraries, Vision Statements, and Action Reports*

---

## 1. ESSENTIAL UNDERSTANDING(S) & GUIDING QUESTIONS

### Essential Understandings
* What does it mean to listen autonomously to discussions of future goals and ambitions at the A2 level?
* How can a listener select, combine, and evaluate strategies for different oral text types when exploring career visions and life milestones?

### Guiding Questions
* What information structure is typical of personal dream narratives, goal itineraries, and action reports?
* Which notebook organizer and dual-coding format best fits the listening purpose for tracking ambitions?
* When is inference necessary in interpreting a speaker's underlying motivation, and how can it be verified?
* How does collaborative jigsaw synthesis demonstrate independent strategy selection and reflection?

---

## 2. LEARNING OUTCOMES

* **Identify Text Types & Purposes:** Recognize personal narratives, goal itineraries, and action reports to select suitable listening purposes and notebook organizers.
* **Integrate Listening Modes:** Combine global, selective, and inferential listening with increasing autonomy across multi-speaker audio tracks.
* **Recognize Connected Speech & Connectors:** Identify discourse markers (addition, contrast, sequence, cause, result) and connected-speech cues (`gonna`, `wanna`, `I'll`, `plan on`) that organize meaning.
* **Verify Evidence & Justify Interpretations:** Verify notebook notes and justify role-specific interpretations with specific audio evidence.
* **Complete Collaborative Deliverables:** Complete team-based synthesis and metacognitive reflections on strategy decisions (Hoja CAADI).

---
## 3. KEY CONCEPTS AND VOCABULARY

* **Autonomy & Strategy Selection:** Informed choice of listening modes and organizers without over-reliance on teacher instruction.
* **Future Ambitions & Career Fields:** Bilingual education, eco-architecture, international humanitarian nursing, and serious educational game development.
* **Discourse & Connectors:** Sequence markers (*first, next, after graduation, by year five*), contrast markers (*however, although, nevertheless*), and additions.
* **Connected Speech Features:** Weak forms (`/ˈɡənə/`, `/ˈwɑːnə/`), contractions (`/aɪl/`, `/aɪv/`), and consonant-to-vowel linkings.
* **Multimodal Dual-Coding:** Integrating analytical written notes with conceptual visual drawings (*Vision Emblem, Milestone Journey Map, Hurdle Shield, Action Toolkit*).
* **Self-Monitoring & Evidence Check:** Tracking local comprehension breakdowns, recording audio quotes, and completing metacognitive debriefs.

---
## 4. MAIN CHAPTER CONTENT

### Autonomy is Informed Choice
Autonomous listening does not mean listening alone without support. It means that the learner can identify a purpose, select a strategy, monitor comprehension, and make an adjustment. At the A2 level, this autonomy is developing. You may still use teacher guidance and repeated listening, but you should be able to explain why a timeline, table, route map, or keyword list is appropriate.

Begin by identifying the text type. A personal dream narrative presents long-term ambitions and core values. A goal itinerary connects chronological milestones and timeframes (*within 18 months, by year five*). An action report presents concrete daily habits, required credentials, and resource audits. Text type predicts structure, but the audio must confirm it.

### Match the Organizer to the Message
To reduce cognitive load and organize complex oral texts, combine analytical writing with visual dual-coding in your notebook:
* **For Vision & Purpose:** Use a core mission box and draw the *Vision Emblem* (a symbolic crest capturing the soul of the dream).
* **For Timelines & Milestones:** Use a chronological sequence table and draw the *Milestone Journey Map* (a roadmap with dated checkpoints).
* **For Obstacles & Contrasts:** Use a challenge-countermeasure grid and draw the *Hurdle Shield & Solution Bridge* (barriers on the left, bridge solutions on the right).
* **For Action Steps & Resources:** Use an action habit list and draw the *Action Toolkit / Gear Chest* (icons of tools, credentials, and peer networks).

Start with a simple structure and add categories only when the task requires it. Leave space for corrections because later information may revise earlier notes.

### Combine Listening Modes Strategically
Use global listening during the first uninterrupted playback to establish the situation, speaker identities, and central points. Use selective listening during segmented playback to locate task-relevant details (*budgets, timeframes, degrees, hurdles*). Use inferential listening only when the answer is not stated directly, such as interpreting a speaker's emotional commitment.

Monitor comprehension during listening. If you miss one word but understand the general idea, continue. If you lose the sequence, mark the location in your notes and recover at the next connector. If multiple speakers become confused, label them immediately. Self-monitoring keeps a local problem from becoming a complete breakdown.

### Use Connectors and Speech Features as Navigation
Connectors show addition, contrast, sequence, cause, and result. Connected-speech awareness helps you recognize common chunks. Rather than trying to decode every reduced word, focus on stressed content words and the relationships signaled by connectors. In *"We planned to finish our degree in two years, but the funding was delayed, so we applied for grants,"* `but` introduces the obstacle and `so` introduces the countermeasure.

## 5. PROJECT & TEAM COLLABORATION WORKFLOW

Listening Comprehension Projects should demonstrate independent strategy selection and collaborative interdependence. Operating in teams of four, each student assumes a distinct specialized listening role:
* **Role 1 (Vision & Purpose Anchor):** Captures core dreams, personal motivations, and visualizes the *Vision Emblem*.
* **Role 2 (Timeline & Milestone Cartographer):** Tracks temporal markers, target deadlines, and maps the *Milestone Journey Map*.
* **Role 3 (Obstacle & Contrast Detective):** Notes contrast markers (*however, although*), roadblocks, and phonetic weak forms (*gonna, wanna*), visualizing the *Hurdle Shield*.
* **Role 4 (Action Steps & Resource Auditor):** Records daily habits, credentials, and equipment, illustrating the *Action Toolkit / Gear Chest*.

During the collaboration phase, teams conduct round-robin briefings using structured sentence stems, synthesize individual notes, and evaluate plan feasibility into a unified team *Future Blueprint*.

---

## 6. TRANSFER ACROSS TEXT TYPES & METACOGNITION

A final autonomous check is transfer. Ask whether the chosen strategy would help in a new but related situation. A milestone timeline used for a career dream may also support a travel itinerary or a business startup plan. The strategy is transferable when it responds to the information structure, not only to one topic.

By the end of the unit, you should approach a new A2 audio with a plan: identify the text type, choose a purpose and organizer, listen in layers, monitor difficulty, verify evidence, and reflect on the result through Hoja CAADI.
