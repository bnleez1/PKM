- Choose one of the three options for completing this task.

    Here are three options for lesson plans that incorporate the specified grammar structures (A2-B1 level) within an ePortfolio context, designed for thoughtful reflection and insight. Each option focuses on a different theme and incorporates a variety of activities to engage learners. I've emphasized the reflective and insightful aspects, which are crucial for deeper learning and ePortfolio development.

    **Option 1: "My Life's Journey" E-Portfolio**

    - **Theme:** Personal narrative and growth over time. This option connects strongly with your personal experiences.

    - **Overall Goal:** Create an ePortfolio that documents your personal journey, highlighting key moments, challenges, learning experiences, and future aspirations. It's essentially an evolving autobiography.

    - **Sections (and corresponding grammar structures):**

        1. **"My Daily Routine" (Simple Present/Continuous):**

            - Describe your typical daily routine (work, study, hobbies). Use both simple present (for habitual actions) and present continuous (for actions happening now or around the time of speaking).

            - **Reflection Prompt:** "How has your routine changed over time? What aspects of your routine would you *like* to change, and why?" (This introduces "would like to" + infinitive, a related structure).

        2. **"Challenges I Face" (Modal Verbs: can, could, have to, must):**

            - Discuss everyday challenges you encounter (e.g., balancing work and study, managing time, dealing with stress). Use modal verbs to express ability, possibility, obligation, and necessity.

            - **Reflection Prompt:** "Which challenge is the most difficult for you to overcome, and why? How *could* you approach this challenge differently in the future?"

        3. **"My Travel Experiences" (Simple Past & Present Perfect):**

            - Share stories about past travel experiences (using simple past) and discuss places they *have visited* or *haven't visited yet* (using present perfect). Include photos, videos, or short audio recordings.

            - **Reflection Prompt:** "What is the most important thing you *have learned* from your travels? How *has* traveling changed your perspective?"

        4. **"Healthy Habits" (Modal "Should"):**

            - Reflect on your own health and fitness habits, giving advice to themselves (or a friend) using "should" and "shouldn't."

            - **Reflection Prompt:** "What are three things you *should* do to improve your health? What are three things you *shouldn't* do? Be honest with yourself!"

        5. **"My Shopping Habits and Finances" (Quantifiers):**

            - Describe your spending habits and how you manage their money. Use quantifiers (some, any, much, many, a lot of, a few, a little, enough).

            - **Reflection Prompt:** "Are you generally good at managing your money? Do you spend *too much* on anything? Do you have *enough* money for the things you need? What are *some* ways you could improve your financial habits?"

        6. **"Technology in My Life" (First Conditional):**

            - Discuss how you use technology and make predictions about how technology will impact their lives. Focus on "If...then" statements.

            - **Reflection Prompt:** "If you *could* invent a new technology, what would it be and why? What problems *will* it solve?"

        7. **"Future Plans and Goals" (Simple Future & Present Continuous for Future):**

            - Students outline their future plans (using "will" and "going to") and scheduled events (using present continuous).

            - **Reflection Prompt:** "What are you most looking forward to in the future? What steps are you *taking* now to achieve your goals?"

    - **E-Portfolio Platform:** Google Sites, Wix, Weebly, WordPress (free versions), or even a shared Google Doc or presentation.

    **Option 2: "The Advice Columnist" E-Portfolio**

    - **Theme:** Problem-solving and giving advice. This option encourages critical thinking and empathetic communication.

    - **Overall Goal:** Create an ePortfolio that acts as an "advice column" where you respond to various scenarios and questions, using the target grammar structures.

    - **Sections (and corresponding grammar structures):**

        1. **"Ask the Expert (Work/Education)" (Simple Present/Continuous):**

            - *Teacher provides scenarios:* "I'm struggling to balance my work and studies. I work part-time, and I'm *feeling* overwhelmed."

            - *Student Response:* Offer advice using simple present and continuous. "You *work* part-time, so you *need* to manage your time carefully. What *are* you *doing* to relax?"

        2. **"Everyday Dilemmas" (Modal Verbs):**

            - *Teacher provides scenarios:* "I *can't* decide whether to take a new job offer. It *would* be a great opportunity, but I'm scared of change."

            - *Student Response:* Use modal verbs to express possibility, advice, and obligation. "You *could* talk to someone who works there. You *must* weigh the pros and cons."

        3. **"Travel Troubles" (Simple Past & Present Perfect):**

            - *Teacher provides scenarios:* "I *went* to Paris last year, and I *had* a terrible experience with the language barrier. I *haven't traveled* internationally since."

            - *Student Response:* Use simple past and present perfect to offer advice and share similar experiences. "I *have been* to Paris too! You *should have* taken a phrasebook."

        4. **"Health and Wellness Corner" (Modal "Should"):**

            - *Teacher provides scenarios:* "I want to get in better shape, but I don't know where to start."

            - *Student Response:* Give advice using "should." "You *should* start with small changes. You *shouldn't* try to do too much at once."

        5. **"Money Matters" (Quantifiers):**

            - *Teacher provides scenarios:* "I spend *too much* money on clothes. I don't have *enough* money saved for emergencies."

            - *Student Response:* Use quantifiers in their advice. "You need to create a budget. Spend *less* money on things you don't need."

        6. **"Tech Troubleshooter" (First Conditional):**

            - *Teacher provides scenarios:* "If I upgrade my phone, I *will* have to spend a lot of money."

            - *Student Response:* Use the first conditional to explore consequences and offer solutions. "If you *wait* a few months, the price *will* probably go down."

        7. **"Future Planning Forum" (Simple Future & Present Continuous for Future):**

            - *Teacher provides scenarios:* "I *am graduating* from college next year. I *don't know* what I *will* do next."

            - *Student Response:* Use simple future and present continuous to offer suggestions and plans. "You *are going to* have many options. What *are* you *thinking* of doing?"

    - **E-Portfolio Platform:** Similar to Option 1, but consider platforms that allow for comments and interaction (like a blog format) to simulate a real advice column.

    **Option 3: "My Community, My World" E-Portfolio**

    - **Theme:** Exploring local and global issues, fostering civic engagement. This option encourages students to connect their learning to the wider world.

    - **Overall Goal:** Create an ePortfolio that examines issues within their own community and connects them to broader global themes, using the target grammar structures.

    - **Sections (and corresponding grammar structures):**

        1. **"Daily Life in [My City/Town]" (Simple Present/Continuous):**

            - Describe aspects of daily life in their community (e.g., transportation, education, local businesses).

            - **Reflection Prompt:** "What are the best and worst things about daily life in your community? What *is happening* now that is changing your community?"

        2. **"Local Challenges" (Modal Verbs):**

            - Identify challenges faced by their community (e.g., traffic congestion, lack of affordable housing, pollution).

            - **Reflection Prompt:** "What *can* be done to address these challenges? What *must* the local government do?"

        3. **"Community Stories" (Simple Past & Present Perfect):**

            - Interview two people you know from your family or community. Present their stories using simple past and present perfect.

            - **Reflection Prompt:** "How *has* your community changed over time? What are some important stories that *have shaped* its identity?"

        4. **"Healthy Communities" (Modal "Should"):**

            - Discuss what makes a community healthy (e.g., access to healthcare, green spaces, safe neighborhoods).

            - **Reflection Prompt:** "What *should* be done to improve the health and well-being of your community?"

        5. **"Local Economy" (Quantifiers):**

            - Explore the local economy, discussing businesses, jobs, and economic opportunities. They use quantifiers to describe the situation.

            - **Reflection Prompt:** "Are there *enough* jobs in your community? Are there *many* opportunities for young people? Is there *too much* reliance on one industry?"

        6. **"Technology and Community" (First Conditional):**

            - Discuss how technology is impacting their community (e.g., access to information, social connections, online services).

            - **Reflection Prompt:** "If your community had access to better technology, how *would* it improve people's lives? If people rely *too much* on technology, what *will* be the consequences?"

        7. **"Future of My Community" (Simple Future & Present Continuous for Future):**

            - Make predictions about the future of their community and discuss plans for development.

            - **Reflection Prompt:** "What *will* your community look like in 10 years? What changes *are happening* now that will shape its future?"

    - **E-Portfolio Platform:** Similar to previous options, but consider platforms that allow for embedding maps, videos, and other multimedia to showcase the community.

# Assessment

- Rubric

    Here's a holistic rubric designed to assess the ePortfolios created in any of the three lesson plan options. This rubric focuses on overall quality and effectiveness, rather than breaking down each grammar point individually. It emphasizes the integration of grammar, content, reflection, and ePortfolio design. I've included levels for "Exceeds Expectations," "Meets Expectations," "Approaches Expectations," and "Needs Improvement" to provide clear differentiation.
