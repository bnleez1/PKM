# Writing II Module 8

## Tuesday

Saint Patrick's Day

- Did you know…?

    ### **How do parades, public festivals, and Irish cultural organizations shape the way St. Patrick’s Day is celebrated in the United States?**

    In the U.S., public events are the main driver of how the holiday looks and feels. Big city parades (often organized with help from Irish-American groups, labor unions, churches, and local governments) turn the day into a highly visible, community-wide celebration. Irish cultural organizations use the holiday to highlight Irish heritage through music, dance, language, and history programming, which helps frame St. Patrick’s Day as both a cultural festival and a community identity event, not only a party.

    ### **Why is wearing green and using shamrock or leprechaun imagery such a common tradition in U.S. St. Patrick’s Day celebrations, and what do these symbols represent?**

    Wearing green became popular partly because green is associated with Ireland (“the Emerald Isle”) and Irish nationalism, and it is an easy, visible way to show participation. The **shamrock** is linked to Irish identity and is commonly connected to St. Patrick traditions (often explained as representing teaching about the Trinity in Christian folklore). **Leprechauns** come from Irish folklore, but in the U.S. they are often used in a simplified, playful way for costumes, decorations, and children’s events. Overall, these symbols work well in American celebrations because they are instantly recognizable and easy to use in public and commercial settings.

    ### **How have U.S. St. Patrick’s Day celebrations changed over time, especially in terms of commercialization, tourism, and alcohol-focused events?**

    Over time, many U.S. celebrations shifted from immigrant-community gatherings and religious observances to large public festivals that attract broad audiences. Cities market parades and related events to bring in tourists, and businesses promote themed products, decorations, and special menus. Alcohol-focused traditions (like pub crawls) became especially prominent because bars and brands use the holiday for major promotions. At the same time, there has also been a counter-movement in some places to emphasize cultural and family-friendly programming, such as Irish music performances, step dancing, and heritage education, to balance the party image with cultural roots.

## Instruction

- Use a **transition** at the start of each body paragraph to show the relationship to the previous idea (addition, contrast, cause/effect, example).

- Use **“this shows…” / “this matters because…”** sentences after evidence to explain your reasoning.

- Use linking sentences to connect to the main idea of the next body paragraph or summarizing sentences to connect back to the thesis statement.

## Instruction

- Start with a **topic sentence** that links back to the thesis.

- Add **evidence** (parenthetical citation, paraphrased, and avoid mentioning the source) with a signal phrase and citation.

- Explain with **“This means…” / “This matters because…”** so the reader understands your reasoning.

- End with a **linking sentence** that sets up the next idea.

- *Make sure each body paragraph has 5-8 sentences, between 125-175 words.*


💡

When writing an expository essay, aim for one clear claim (A Claim) per paragraph and build it with a consistent pattern.

- Example of paraphrasing

    ### Direct quote (from a freely available public article)

    “Teachers are already integrating AI into the classroom in fun, engaging, and academically rich ways—by having students fact-check ChatGPT’s responses, for instance…” (Edutopia, n.d.).

    ### Three paraphrases (same idea), each with a *parenthetical* citation

    1. Many classrooms are already using AI in enjoyable and meaningful ways, ~~including~~ which include having students verify the accuracy of AI-generated answers (Edutopia, 2026).

    2. AI is being used in lessons in ways that are both motivating and academically useful, such as activities where students check whether an AI tool is correct (Edutopia, 2026).

    3. Some teachers bring AI into class through engaging tasks that support learning; for example, they ask students to evaluate and fact-check AI responses (Edutopia, 2026).

    ### Reference

    Nichols, H. (2026). *9 tips for using AI for learning (and fun!)* Edutopia. [https://www.edutopia.org/article/using-ai-for-learning-fun/](https://www.edutopia.org/article/using-ai-for-learning-fun/)
