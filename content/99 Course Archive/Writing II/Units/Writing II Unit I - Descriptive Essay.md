# Writing II Unit I - Descriptive Essay

## 🎯 Stage 1 — Desired Results

### A. Established Goals (from course & unit documentation)

- **Course objective**: By the end of the course, students compose paragraphs and essays in English at **CEFR B1+**. 

- **Unit focus (25 hours)**: Pre‑writing (topic focus, brainstorming, graphic organizers, outlines), drafting (hook, thesis, topic sentences, cohesion/coherence, clarity, style), revising (self/peer/expert; spelling, punctuation, grammar, organization, APA), editing and final draft submission.

- **CEFR alignment**: At **B1** (moving toward B1+), writers produce **straightforward, connected texts** on familiar subjects, linking ideas in a linear sequence; they can signal chronology and describe experiences with simple detail. These descriptors guide the expected level of clarity, cohesion, and control.

> UbD anchor: Unit design will follow UbD’s Stage 1–3 sequence—identify desired results → determine acceptable evidence → plan learning—with transfer and meaning at the core.

### B. Transfer Goals

Students will independently use their learning to:

1. **Plan and produce** a multi‑paragraph **descriptive essay** that clearly serves an authentic audience/purpose, with unity, coherence, and appropriate detail.

2. **Apply a repeatable revision process** to improve sentence clarity, paragraph focus, and cohesion before submitting polished work.

3. **Write at B1+**, making mostly accurate grammar and vocabulary choices so that meaning remains clear to the reader.

### C. Meaning

**Enduring Understandings (Students will understand that …)**

- Effective description **selects and organizes sensory detail** to serve a **clear purpose and audience**, not to list everything observed.

- **Coherence and cohesion** emerge from **topic sentences, logical sequencing, and transitions**, not from adding more words.

- **Revision is central** to writing; clarity improves when writers re‑examine organization, sentence structure, and word choice before editing surface errors.

- **Academic integrity** applies to process and product; unauthorized use of GenAI constitutes plagiarism for this course.

**Essential Questions**

1. **What makes description vivid yet purposeful** rather than a string of observations?

2. **How do organization and sentence choices** help readers see, hear, and feel what I intend?

3. **Where in my process** does revision most improve clarity—and how do I know?

### D. Acquisition

**Students will know …**

- Features of descriptive writing (focus/unity, sensory domains, precise vocabulary, figurative language used judiciously, paragraph structure).

- Organizational tools: **brainstorming**, **graphic organizers**, **outlines** to plan before drafting.

- Drafting elements: **hooks**, **thesis/main idea**, **topic sentences**, transitions for cohesion and coherence. 

- Revising & editing criteria (organization, clarity, grammar/punctuation, APA as required). 

- CEFR B1+ expectations for connected, straightforward prose and control of common structures.

**Students will be able to …**

- **Focus and specify** a descriptive topic; **organize** ideas visually and linearly before drafting.

- **Draft** an introduction with a clear focus, **develop** body paragraphs with concrete detail and cohesion, and **conclude** by reinforcing the central impression.

- **Revise** for unity, coherence, and clarity (moving from global to sentence‑level concerns), then **edit** for correctness and APA requirements.

- Produce writing that meets **B1+** readability: mostly accurate grammar and vocabulary with few errors that impede understanding. 

## 🎯 Stage 2 — Evidence

### A. Performance Task (GRASPS)

- **Goal**: Craft a descriptive “profile” that lets readers **experience** a significant space/object/person from your context.

- **Role**: You are a contributor to a department microsite showcasing authentic campus and community spaces for incoming ELT students.

- **Audience**: New BA‑ELT students exploring the setting in English.

- **Situation**: The site needs a **600–800‑word** descriptive essay that **shows** (not lists) details and maintains unity/coherence.

- **Product/Performance**:

    1. **Planning artifacts** (brainstorming, graphic organizer, outline).

    2. **Draft** with clear paragraphs (intro → body → conclusion).

    3. **Revised draft** demonstrating global and sentence‑level improvements.

    4. **Edited final** in APA format (as required in course) with a 150–200 word **process reflection** identifying the 2–3 most impactful revisions. 

- **Standards/Rubric (summary)**:

    - **Purpose & audience**: central impression is clear and sustained.

    - **Organization & cohesion**: paragraph unity, logical sequencing, effective transitions.

    - **Detail & diction**: precise vocabulary, concrete sensory detail, appropriate figurative language.

    - **Language control (B1+)**: mostly accurate grammar and vocabulary; occasional errors do not impede meaning.

    - **Process & integrity**: complete planning/revision evidence; complies with course integrity policy regarding GenAI.

### B. Other Evidence

- **Quick writes** and **micro‑descriptions** targeting specific senses (checked for unity and specificity).

- **Organizer/outlining checks** with peer feedback notes.

- **Paragraph diagnostics** (topic sentence identification, transition effectiveness).

- **Revision checklists** and **self‑assessments** (global → local).

- **Exit tickets** tied to essential questions (e.g., "What did you revise to sharpen the central impression today?").
