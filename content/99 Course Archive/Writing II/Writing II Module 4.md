- Example body paragraph

    The abandoned lighthouse on the rocky coast stands as a weathered sentinel, its peeling paint and broken windows telling stories of storms weathered and ships guided safely home.

    The abandoned lighthouse that rises above the sharp rocks keeps watch like an old guard, even though it is falling apart. Lighthouses often stand in dangerous places, like rocky coasts and near harbors, so their lights can warn sailors about danger and help ships find safe water (NOAA National Ocean Service, n.d.). This explains why the peeling paint and broken windows look like signs of many years of wind, salt, and storms. In the past, lighthouses were built to guide ships safely into busy ports and away from hidden rocks (National Park Service, n.d.). Because of this, the lighthouse feels like more than an empty building; it feels like a strong protector with a long history. Even now, its damaged look still suggests it is watching over the coast.

    Weather and time have left their marks on the tower, with flaking layers and shattered panes hinting at years of harsh winds and powerful storms. Lighthouses are typically built in high-risk coastal locations where strong winds, salt spray, and severe storms are common, which helps explain why the structure’s worn surfaces and broken glass suggest years of heavy weathering (NOAA National Ocean Service, n.d.).

        1. What is the main idea of essay?

        2. What is the central question the essay sets out to answer? How? Why? etc.

- Example of an introduction paragraph for a descriptive (five-paragraph) essay

    “The sea, once it casts its spell, holds one in its net of wonder forever.” — Jacques-Yves Cousteau. On the far edge of town, where the pavement ends and the sea wind begins, an abandoned lighthouse still stands like a tired guardian watching over the rocks below. Although its cracked paint, rusted railings, and broken windows make it look forgotten, lighthouses were originally built to guide ships away from danger and toward safe water, especially near hazardous coastlines and busy harbors (NOAA National Ocean Service, n.d.). This history helps explain why the lighthouse feels both lonely and important, as if it carries the weight of countless storms and silent rescues.  The abandoned lighthouse shows its weathered appearance, creates a powerful sensory atmosphere, and symbolizes a lasting protector of the coast.
