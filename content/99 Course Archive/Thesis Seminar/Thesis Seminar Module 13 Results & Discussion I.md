### 🎯 Understanding

**Instructors will understand that…**

- The Results and Discussion section is typically written as one unified section: **Results** (what the data shows) + **Discussion** (what it means).

- Clear organization (by research question, themes, chronology, cases, etc.) makes findings easier to follow and more persuasive.

- Strong Results/Discussion writing balances **evidence** (quotes, observations, counts, visuals) with **analysis** (interpretation, implications, links to literature).

### 🎯 Language Objective

**Students will be able to…**

- Distinguish between **results** and **discussion** in sample excerpts and label each accurately.

- Draft 2–4 Level II subsection headings for their own Results & Discussion section and justify the organizing principle.

- Write a MEAL-style paragraph that presents **results first** and then **discusses/interprets** those results (with at least one citation/connection to the literature review).

### 🎯 Vocabulary

- results (findings; what you found)

- discussion (interpretation; what it means)

- evidence (data extract, quote, frequency, observation)

- analysis / interpretation

- implication

- theme (coding category)

- subsection / heading (Level II heading)

- triangulation

- compare / contrast with literature
