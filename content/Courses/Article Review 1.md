# Article Review 1

## Purpose

Write a **250–300 word review of one peer-reviewed empirical research article** related to your developing research topic.

Your review should explain:

**What problem was investigated? → How was it investigated? → What was discovered? → Why does it matter for my research?**

The goal is to show that you can understand, accurately report, and evaluate research rather than summarize every part of an article.

---

## 1. Learning Outcomes

By the end of this learning experience, students will be able to:

- identify and accurately report the main problem, purpose, methodology, findings, and conclusions of a research article;

- explain how the article contributes to their own developing research topic; and

- use appropriate academic language, reporting tense, and cautious claims when discussing research.

---

## 2. Learning Context and Content

## Essential Content

Students will draw upon:

- the **Student Guide: Writing a 250–300 Word Article Review**;

- a peer-reviewed empirical research article related to their research topic;

- appropriate reporting tense, modal verbs, and hedging;

- APA in-text citation and reference conventions.

## Prior Knowledge

Students should already be able to:

- identify the basic sections of an empirical research article;

- identify a developing research topic or problem;

- paraphrase information from a source.

## Connection to Learning

This assignment helps students begin identifying and evaluating research that may later be used in their **annotated bibliography and literature review**.

---

## 3. Apply & Create

### Central Task

Write a **250–300 word article review** organized into four short paragraphs.

### Students will:

1. **Problem and Purpose — about 50–60 words**

    Explain the problem, importance, purpose, and research question(s).

2. **Methodology — about 40–50 words**

    Identify the participants/context, research design, and main data-collection method(s).

3. **Major Findings — about 70–80 words**

    Explain two or three important findings and distinguish findings from interpretation.

4. **Conclusions and Relevance — about 70–90 words**

    Explain the authors’ conclusion and an important limitation, implication, or recommendation. Explain why the article matters to your own research.

### Interaction:

**Individual**

**Time:** 40–50 minutes

---

# 4. Evidence of Learning

## Student Evidence

Students will produce:

- one **250–300 word article review**;

- an APA reference for the reviewed article;

- a revised final draft.

### Evidence Check

The review should demonstrate that the student understands:

- what the study investigated;

- why it was conducted;

- how it was conducted;

- what the researchers found;

- why the study is relevant to the student's research.

---

# 5. Assessment & Feedback

**Assessment purpose:** Summative with formative feedback during drafting.

## Success Criteria

Students demonstrate successful learning when they can:

1. accurately explain the problem, purpose, methodology, findings, and conclusion;

2. select important information rather than summarize the entire article;

3. explain the article's relevance to their own developing research;

4. use appropriate academic language, APA citation, reporting tense, and cautious claims.

# 7. Responsible GenAI Use

GenAI should have an **explicit pedagogical purpose**. It should support learning without replacing the thinking, decision-making, or performance students are expected to demonstrate.

## AI Use Level

Select the appropriate level:

- [x] **No GenAI** — Students complete the experience without GenAI.

- [x] **Optional GenAI Support** — Students may use GenAI for specified supporting purposes.

- [ ] **Structured GenAI Use** — Students use GenAI at designated stages and according to specific instructions.

- [x] **Critical GenAI Engagement** — Students analyze, evaluate, verify, challenge, or revise AI-generated material.

- [x] **Required GenAI Collaboration** — Interaction with GenAI is itself part of the intended learning experience.

---

## Permitted Uses

Students may use GenAI to:

- [x] Brainstorm

- [x] Generate questions

- [x] Clarify concepts

- [ ] Receive formative feedback

- [x] Compare alternatives

- [x] Practice language

- [x] Generate an alternative perspective

- [x] Revise after producing an initial version

- [x] Critique AI-generated content

- [x] Other: __________

## Restricted Uses

GenAI may **not**:

- read and analyze the article instead of the student;

- decide which findings are most important;

- determine the article's relevance to the student's research;

- write the initial article review.

---

## Cognitive Ownership

The student's own **reading, interpretation, evaluation, selection of evidence, and initial drafting** must remain independent.

# Final Design Check

Before submission, students should confirm:

- My review is **250–300 words**.

- I explain the research problem and purpose.

- I briefly describe the methodology.

- I explain two or three major findings.

- I include the authors’ conclusion and/or an important limitation, implication, or recommendation.

- I explain why the article is relevant to my research.

- I mostly paraphrase instead of quote.

- I use appropriate APA citation.

- I use reporting tense and cautious language appropriately.

- The initial draft represents my own reading, thinking, and writing.

- If I used GenAI, I documented how I used it.

This version keeps the **same 11 sections and final design check from the Article Review 1 PDF**, but removes most of the generic instructional-design language that students do not need. It also makes the four-paragraph structure from the student guide the actual central task rather than treating the guide as a separate resource.
