## Wednesday

## Instruction - Evidence That Supports a Counterclaim

1. Paraphrase *ideas* (not words) accurately and ethically.

2. Integrate paraphrases as evidence sentences for the counterclaim body paragraph.

3. Add correct APA parenthetical citations to every paraphrase.

- Spot the paraphrase

    **Original source sentence**

    - “Regular physical activity can improve students’ concentration and reduce stress during exam periods” (Lopez, 2022, p. 2).

    **Student Version A (too close / patchwriting)**

    - Physical activity improves concentration and reduces stress during exams (Lopez, 2022).

    **Student Version B (acceptable paraphrase)**

    - Students who exercise consistently may focus better and feel less stressed when exams are approaching (Lopez, 2022).

    **Student Version C (changes meaning / inaccurate)**

    - Exercise completely eliminates stress for all students during exams (Lopez, 2022).

    **Quick class question**

    - Which version is the best paraphrase: A, B, or C? Why?

- The Three Switches of the Strong Paraphrase - Simple Checklist

    - [ ]  **Structure switch:** My sentence structure is clearly different from the source.

    - [ ]  **Vocabulary switch:** I avoided copying unique phrases (I used my own wording).

    - [ ]  **Focus switch:** I changed what comes first (cause/effect, general/specific, concession, etc.).

    - [ ]  **Accuracy:** My paraphrase keeps the same meaning and level of certainty.

    - [ ]  **Key terms:** I kept any technical terms/names that should not change or I avoided key terms but said the same thing in my own words.

    - [ ]  **APA citation:** I added a parenthetical citation right after the paraphrase (Author, Year).

## Thursday

## Instruction - Evidence That Supports a Counterclaim

1. Develop second body paragraph according to the The M.E.A.L. Plan: A Comprehensive Approach to Paragraph Development and APA Guide.

    1. **Topic sentence** should support the counterclaim (opposed of the topic sentence of your initial claim from the first body paragraph.

        1. Begins with the main clause.

        2. Avoid linking verbs like, “to be”, etc. in the main clause - Copula (linking) verbs

        3. Avoid words like, “very”, “important”, etc.

    2. Use **parenthetical citations** where you paraphrased someone else’s ideas (from yesterday’s lesson). See **Parenthetical Vs. Narrative Citations**.

    3. Begin your body paragraph with **M.E.** and conclude with **A.L.**

    4. Include 5-8 sentences between **120-180 words**.

    5. Include **one-to-two evidence sentences** using parenthetical citations that are paraphrasing someone’s ideas - avoid direct quotes.

    6. Include **2-3 additional uses of the comma** that you did not use in your first body paragraph.
