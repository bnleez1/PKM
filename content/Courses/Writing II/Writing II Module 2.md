### Group feedback based on those who posted to Teams

Thanks to those who shared your thesis statement yesterday in Teams. Everyone should check your thesis statements for the following:

1. Your thesis statement is one sentence.

2. Your sentence begins with a strong topic (or subject) that includes at least one adjective (‣) and one prepositional phrase (Prepositional phrases).

3. You may use one of the verbs from our list on Tuesday as your main verb (instead of the verb, "to be").

4. Avoid the second person. See ‣.

5. When writing your thesis statement, try to avoid the following:

    1. Subject pronouns as the subject

    2. The verb, "to be" as your main verb

    3. Words like "very" and "important", and 4) "There is/are".

6. Include your thesis statement at the top of your Word document in Descriptive Essays.

- Review Thesis statement: transition, topic, A Claim, connector or preposition like “because” or “by”, and two-to-four key points.

- Examples of thesis statements for a descriptive essay

    Subject - Verb

    First key point - Second key point - Third key point

    - In addition to verbs like, “to stand”, “to be”, and “to awaken”, consider other verbs as well

        1. **Embodies** - captures the essence of something

        2. **Reveals** - uncovers or shows characteristics

        3. **Represents** - stands for or symbolizes

        4. **Displays** - exhibits or shows

        5. **Captures** - holds or seizes attention/qualities

        6. **Reflects** - mirrors or shows back

        7. **Evokes** - calls forth feelings or images

        8. **Radiates** - emanates or projects

        9. **Exudes** - gives off or emanates

        10. **Transforms** - changes or converts

        11. **Invites** - welcomes or draws in

        12. **Emerges** - comes forth or appears

        13. **Unfolds** - opens up or reveals gradually

        14. **Breathes** - gives life or animates

        15. **Whispers** - suggests subtly

        16. **Pulses** - throbs or vibrates with

        17. **Sprawls** - spreads out

        18. **Beckons** - calls or attracts

        19. **Cradles** - holds gently or protectively

        20. **Nestles** - settles comfortably

        21. **Dominates** - commands or controls

        22. **Envelops** - surrounds or wraps

        23. **Resonates** - echoes or vibrates

        24. **Thrives** - flourishes or prospers

        25. **Enchants** - captivates or charms

    1. **The abandoned lighthouse on the rocky coast stands as a weathered sentinel, its peeling paint and broken windows telling stories of storms weathered and ships guided safely home.**

        💡 Each topic sentence establishes the "territory" (the lighthouse as sentinel) and sets up the paragraph to zoom into the "niche" with specific sensory details about its weathered condition and protective role.

        1. **The lighthouse rises from the jagged rocks like a solitary guard who has refused to abandon its post despite decades of neglect.**

        2. **Standing tall against the relentless ocean winds, the lighthouse embodies the steadfast vigilance of a soldier who has witnessed countless battles.**

        3. **With its crumbling tower reaching skyward, the lighthouse maintains its watchful presence over the churning waters below, a silent protector weathered by time but unbroken in purpose.**

    2. **My grandmother's kitchen is a warm sanctuary filled with the aroma of freshly baked bread, the gentle hum of her old refrigerator, and walls adorned with faded photographs that chronicle decades of family gatherings.**

    3. **The bustling farmers market on Sunday morning awakens the senses with vibrant displays of colorful produce, the cheerful banter of vendors calling out their specials, and the mingling scents of fresh herbs and artisan breads.**

    These thesis statements work well for descriptive essays because they:

    - Clearly identify the subject being described

    - Establish a dominant impression or mood

    - Use sensory details and vivid language

    - Set the tone for the descriptive essay that follows

## Thursday

> **Why Descriptive Writing Matters**: Descriptive writing builds foundational skills that extend far beyond this unit. The ability to observe carefully, select relevant detail, and organize information coherently transfers to research writing, where you must describe methods, settings, and results with precision. It supports critical reading, as you learn to notice how published authors craft descriptions for rhetorical effect. And it deepens your awareness of **audience** and **purpose**—the twin anchors of all academic writing.
As you move through Unit I, remember that effective description is purposeful, organized, and grounded in concrete detail. Your task is not to tell readers everything you observe, but to **select and arrange observations** that allow readers to see, hear, and feel what makes your subject worth describing. By revising deliberately and editing carefully, you will produce a 600–800 word descriptive essay that demonstrates B1+ language control while bringing a meaningful campus space to life for your audience (Part I: What Makes a Descriptive Essay?).

    1. A topic sentence begins with a topic (strong subject). Try to avoiding beginning a topic sentence with a transition. See Types of transitions.

    3. A topic sentence captures the main idea of the paragraph.

    1. **The abandoned lighthouse on the rocky coast stands as a weathered sentinel, its peeling paint and broken windows telling stories of storms weathered and ships guided safely home.**

        💡 Each topic sentence establishes the "territory" (the lighthouse as sentinel) and sets up the paragraph to zoom into the "niche" with specific sensory details about its weathered condition and protective role.

        1. **The lighthouse rises from the jagged rocks like a solitary guard who has refused to abandon its post despite decades of neglect.**

        2. **Standing tall against the relentless ocean winds, the lighthouse embodies the steadfast vigilance of a soldier who has witnessed countless battles.**

        3. **With its crumbling tower reaching skyward, the lighthouse maintains its watchful presence over the churning waters below, a silent protector weathered by time but unbroken in purpose.**

    These thesis statements work well for descriptive essays because they:

    - Clearly identify the subject being described

    - Establish a dominant impression or mood

    - Use sensory details and vivid language

    - Set the tone for the descriptive essay that follows
