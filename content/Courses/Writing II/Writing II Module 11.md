## Action Steps

1. Evidence should be descriptive by answering any of the following question words: what, why, when, where, how, with whom, etc. Avoid subjective tone: “it is important”, “it is great/good…”

2. Even though evidence should be specific examples, avoid transitional phrases like, “For example…” or “For instance”, etc. Your first evidence sentence does not require a transition, but your second evidence sentence could include an addition-type of sentence connector: *Also, in addition to…, additionally, moreover, furthermore, besides, equally, as well as,* etc.

💡

Today we’ll focus on defining an initial claim, counterclaim, and rebuttal.

1. Review A Claim.

    1. An initial claim is the main position of the writer. The writer is for or against something or they agree or disagree with something.

    2. A counterclaim is to state what is wrong or weak about the initial claim.

    3. A rebuttal is to state what is wrong or weak about the counterclaim.
