# Discourse Analysis Unit II: Making Meaning in Text

## 🎯 Stage 1 — Desired Results

### A. Established Goals

Students will analyze how grammatical choices and vocabulary intersect to build cohesion, coherence, and interpersonal meaning within extended discourse.

### B. Transfer Goals

Students will independently use their learning to:

- Transition their instructional focus from teaching isolated, sentence-level grammar to facilitating discourse-level meaning-making.

- Curate and annotate authentic texts to explicitly show learners how language choices reflect authorial stance and bind ideas together, bringing transparency to the reading and writing process.

### C. Meaning

**Enduring Understandings (Students will understand that …)**

- Grammar is not just a set of rules for sentence construction; it is a vital tool for packaging information (theme and rheme) to guide a reader's attention.

- Tense, aspect, and modality function beyond the sentence level to build narratives, express interpersonal relationships, and reveal the speaker or writer's stance.

- Cohesion and coherence rely heavily on the deliberate intersection of vocabulary and grammatical links.

**Essential Questions**

- How do writers use vocabulary and grammar to glue a text together?

- What do our choices in verb tense and modality reveal about our attitude and relationship with our audience?

- How can we make the invisible threads of a text (theme and rheme) visible to English language learners?

### D. Acquisition

**Students will know …**

- The role of formal links and lexical cohesion in binding texts together.

- The concepts of theme and rheme (information packaging).

- The discourse functions of tense and aspect beyond sentence-level grammar.

- The role of modality in expressing speaker/writer stance and interpersonal relationships.

**Students will be able to …**

- Diagram thematic progression in student-generated and authentic texts.

- Analyze narrative texts to track shifts in tense and aspect and their effect on the reader.

- Identify and evaluate the effectiveness of lexical cohesive devices in varied genres.

- Interpret the degree of formality and authorial stance through the use of modals.

## 🎯 Stage 2 — Evidence

### A. Performance Task (GRASPS)

- **Goal:** Create a "Grammar in Context" toolkit that visually maps out the cohesive devices and interpersonal meanings within an authentic text, shifting the focus from abstract rules to practical discourse application.

- **Role:** A transparent language educator and facilitator.

- **Audience:** The broader public at large and fellow ELT educators looking for practical ways to teach grammar contextually.

- **Situation:** To foster an engaging community of educators, you are developing an open-source teaching resource. You must select a piece of extended discourse (e.g., an op-ed, a short story, or a public speech) and create an annotated guide that deconstructs its grammatical and lexical choices to show exactly *how* the text makes meaning.

- **Product/Performance:** A multimodal annotated text (e.g., an interactive PDF, a video teardown using a digital whiteboard, or a slide deck with voiceover) highlighting lexical cohesion, thematic progression, and modality. The guide must draw upon concepts from Cook (1989) Section 1 and McCarthy (1991) Chapters 2 & 3.

**Standards/Rubric (summary):** (Note: Unit II assignments and quizzes represent 25% of the total course grade.)

- **Purpose & audience:** The toolkit translates complex linguistic theories into accessible, transparent instructional materials for other teachers and learners.

- **Organization & cohesion:** The analysis logically breaks down theme/rheme, tense/aspect, and modality, demonstrating how they build interpersonal meaning.

- **Detail & diction:** Correctly identifies and evaluates lexical cohesive devices and thematic progression in the chosen genre.

- **Language control (B1+):** Employs accurate, professional language; the use of English accounts for 50% of the item's evaluated value.

- **Process & integrity:** Adheres strictly to APA style for all cited sources and references; contains no plagiarized or improperly AI-generated content.

**B. Other Evidence**

- **Formative Quizzes:** Assessing conceptual knowledge of formal links, theme/rheme, and the discourse functions of tense, aspect, and modality.

- **Narrative Shift Journal:** A short written analysis tracking shifts in tense and aspect within a provided narrative text to determine the effect on the reader.

- **Thematic Diagramming Task:** An in-class practical assignment diagramming thematic progression using student-generated texts.
