# Writing I Unit I: Paragraph Foundations

# Writing I — Unit I

- **Subject**: [[Writing I]]

- **UbD Unit Design | 20 hours | Initial–A2 CEFR**

# 🎯 Stage 1 — Desired Results

## A. Established Goals

Students will:

- Apply the **writing process—prewriting, drafting, and revising—to create and organize brief expository and descriptive texts in English at an A2 CEFR level**.

- Organize ideas into clear paragraphs using basic **sentence structures, punctuation, connectors, and paragraph organization**.

- Write about familiar topics connected to **personal background, daily routines, and campus/university life**.

- Use graphic organizers and other planning strategies to develop and organize ideas before writing.

- When authorized by the teacher, use GenAI only to **understand model texts, activate prior knowledge, explore A2 vocabulary, brainstorm, and plan writing**.

- Maintain full authorship of submitted writing. GenAI may not be used to generate sentences or paragraphs for submission, and any authorized AI use must be documented.

## B. Transfer Goals

Students will independently use their learning to:

- Use a writing process to plan, draft, review, and improve short texts in English in new academic and everyday situations.

- Organize ideas so that a reader can easily understand the main idea and supporting information.

- Communicate information about familiar experiences, routines, people, and places using clear A2-level English.

- Make responsible decisions about learning tools, including GenAI, while keeping ownership of their ideas and writing.

## C. Meaning

### Enduring Understandings

**Students will understand that …**

- Good writing usually develops through a **process**; writers plan, draft, review, and improve their work rather than expecting the first draft to be final.

- A clear paragraph helps the reader by presenting ideas in an organized way around a central idea.

- Basic connectors and punctuation help show relationships between ideas and make writing easier to understand.

- Writers can use different planning strategies and graphic organizers to develop the same core writing skills.

- Familiar experiences such as personal background, routines, and university life can provide meaningful material for clear descriptive and expository writing.

- GenAI can support thinking and planning, but it should **not replace the thinking or writing of the student**. The writer remains responsible for choosing ideas and producing the text.

### Essential Questions

- How can I organize my ideas so another person can understand my message?

- How does the writing process help me improve a paragraph?

- How do sentences, connectors, and punctuation make my writing clearer?

- How can I use my own experiences to write meaningful descriptive and expository texts?

- How can GenAI help me prepare to write without doing the writing for me?

## D. Acquisition

### Students will know …

- Vocabulary and common language for discussing:

    - introductions and personal background;

    - daily routines; and

    - campus and university life.

- Basic sentence types and the functions of common punctuation marks.

- The basic organization of a paragraph, including:

    - a main or topic idea;

    - supporting ideas;

    - basic connectors; and

    - an appropriate closing idea.

- Ways of using graphic organizers to plan and organize information.

- The stages of the writing process:

    - **prewriting**;

    - **drafting**; and

    - **revising**.

- Basic features of descriptive writing and the paragraph forms addressed during Unit I.

- That the Unit I syllabus also includes practice with **narrative and descriptive paragraphs**.

- The permitted uses of GenAI in Unit I: understanding models, brainstorming, activating prior knowledge, exploring A2 vocabulary, and planning.

- That authorized GenAI use must be documented by recording the **tool, date, prompts, and ideas accepted, adapted, or rejected**.

### Students will be able to …

- Identify important parts of a model paragraph, including the topic/main idea, supporting ideas, connectors, and closing idea.

- Generate ideas about familiar personal and university-related topics.

- Select relevant ideas and organize them using a graphic organizer.

- Use basic sentence structures and punctuation appropriately.

- Connect related ideas using basic connectors.

- Draft a brief A2-level descriptive or expository text about a familiar topic.

- Review their own writing for meaning, organization, punctuation, and basic language use.

- Revise a draft to make the message clearer and better organized.

- Participate in peer review and use appropriate feedback to improve a text.

- Explain their own writing choices.

- When GenAI has been authorized, evaluate AI-generated ideas and decide which ideas to accept, adapt, or reject before independently writing their text.

UDL is embedded in these outcomes by allowing students to approach content through **model texts, teacher modeling, connector guides, dictionaries, graphic organizers, digital resources, and collaborative work**. Students can select familiar topics and use different forms of planning—for example, a concept map, table, sequence organizer, outline, or handwritten/digital organizer—while the essential assessed construct remains the student's ability to **produce their own written English**.

# 🎯 Stage 2 — Evidence

## A. Performance Task (GRASPS)

### Goal

Create a clear, organized, student-authored **A2-level text about your life as a university student**, using the writing process to develop and improve your ideas.

The text will communicate descriptive and/or explanatory information about a syllabus-supported topic such as:

- your personal background;

- your daily routine; or

- your campus/university life.

### Role

You are a **new university student and English writer** sharing information about yourself and your experience.

### Audience

Your classmates and teacher.

### Situation

You are beginning your university experience and becoming part of a new learning community. Your classmates come with different backgrounds, routines, and experiences. You will use English to communicate something meaningful about yourself or your university life in a way that your classmates can understand.

You will not simply write a final paragraph immediately. You will use the writing process to develop the text: first organizing your ideas, then drafting, reviewing, and revising them.

Students may select from the familiar Unit I topics, supporting engagement through meaningful choice without changing the writing skills being assessed.

### Product/Performance

Students will submit evidence of a **writing process** that includes:

1. **Prewriting/planning** using a graphic organizer or another teacher-approved organizational format.

2. A **student-written draft**.

3. Evidence of **review and revision**.

4. A **revised A2-level descriptive or expository text** using appropriate basic sentences, punctuation, connectors, and paragraph organization.

5. When GenAI was authorized and used, a brief **AI-use record** identifying:

    - the tool;

    - date;

    - prompts used;

    - ideas accepted, adapted, or rejected.

Planning can be completed using different visual or organizational formats, and students may use model texts, dictionaries, connector guides, and teacher/peer support. These UDL options reduce barriers to planning and comprehension without changing the central performance requirement: **the final English text must be written by the student**.

GenAI may support comprehension, vocabulary exploration, brainstorming, or planning **only with teacher authorization**. Students may not ask GenAI to write sentences or paragraphs that are submitted as their own work.

### Standards/Rubric (summary)

- **Purpose and meaning:** The writing communicates a clear message about the selected familiar topic and provides relevant supporting information.

- **Organization:** Ideas are organized around a clear central idea and follow an understandable sequence.

- **Sentence and punctuation control:** The student uses basic sentence structures and punctuation with enough control for the reader to understand the message.

- **Connectors and cohesion:** Basic connectors help show relationships among ideas.

- **Language control — A2:** Vocabulary and structures are appropriate to the student's developing A2 level. The syllabus specifies that **English use represents 50% of the grade for each evaluated item**.

- **Writing process:** Planning, drafting, reviewing, and revising are visible in the submitted evidence.

- **Independent authorship:** The content and language of the submitted text reflect the student's own writing and decisions.

- **Responsible GenAI use:** When GenAI is used, its use stays within the Unit I permissions and is transparently documented. The student can explain what was accepted, changed, or rejected.

- **Originality:** Submitted work follows the syllabus requirement of **90–100% originality** and reflects the student's authentic voice.

## B. Other Evidence

- **Weekly writing tasks** involving personal background, daily routines, and campus/university life.

- Short sentence-building activities focusing on basic sentence types and punctuation.

- Identification of the **main/topic idea, supporting ideas, connectors, and closing idea** in model paragraphs. Students may use visual annotation, teacher modeling, collaborative identification, or—when explicitly authorized—GenAI-assisted model analysis.

- Graphic-organizer activities in which students generate, select, group, and sequence ideas before writing.

- Short descriptive paragraph practice and, consistent with the Unit I content list, **narrative paragraph practice**.

- Draft-to-revision comparisons that show how the student improved organization, clarity, punctuation, or connectors.

- **Peer-feedback and collaborative writing workshops**, with structured criteria appropriate to A2 learners.

- **Self-assessment and reflection** on writing progress, including what students can do independently and what they still need to practice.

- Brief reflection on any authorized GenAI use, especially the student's reasons for **accepting, adapting, or rejecting ideas** generated during brainstorming or planning.

- Participation in individual, pair, and group activities using course-supported resources such as model texts, graphic organizers, connector guides, dictionaries, notebooks, Microsoft Teams, Capacities, and CAADI materials.
